## Core model in one table

| If the pressure is about… | He most likely… | Confidence (evidence) |
|---|---|---|
| **Physics or engineering** (rocket blows up, demo fails) | Stays calm and upbeat, often jokes, gives a root cause within hours to days, names the next date, credits the team | High (Falcon 1 2008, SN8 2020, Cybertruck 2019, Starship 2023–26, Raptor 2021: 8+) |
| **Schedule or competence** ("you're late", "you're dumb") | Concedes as a category ("pathologically optimistic"), points to the hedge, gives a new date | High (A, C, D, G: 15+ instances) |
| **Integrity or motive** ("you lied", "you're malicious, racist, a fraud") | Fights in detail, for years. Trades "stupid" for "not malicious" | High (funding secured, cave diver, DealBook, BBC, Lex #400: 10+) |
| **His words causing a backlash** | Apologizes narrowly for the content, then attacks the people who reacted | High (DealBook 2023, Unsworth 2018, Trump 2025, Broder 2013: 5+) |
| **Institutions using leverage** (regulator, court, advertisers, proxy advisors) | Contempt, then litigation, then exit from the jurisdiction. If cornered: settles tactically and later calls it coercion | High (SEC, Alameda, Delaware, Media Matters, GARM, ISS: 7+) |
| **Betrayal or broken trust** | Exits fast, withdraws the old offer, builds or buys a rival, keeps a long grudge | High (OpenAI 2017–26, Twitter board 2022, advertisers 2023: 4+) |
| **Hostile interviewer** | Stays civil in the room, counter-interrogates, then retaliates on X 0–5 days later | Med-High (Lemon 2024, Pogue 2025, Economist 2026: 3) |
| **Family, his heroes, or harm to his employees** | Visible emotion (voice breaks, pauses). An estranged child's transition becomes a moralized political cause | Med-High (Pelley 2012, NYT 2018, Kudlow 2025, Baier 2025, Peterson 2024: 5) |

## Part 1. Casebook (dated episodes)

### 1. 2008: Falcon 1 failures and double near-bankruptcy
- **Situation.** Flight 3 failed on Aug 2, 2008 (stage collision at separation). Tesla was nearly out of cash. He was going through a divorce.
- **Same night (9:45 PM), email "Plan Going Forward."** One line states the loss, then come positives, then *more* production: he had "given the go ahead to begin fabrication of flight six." It closes with "For my part, I will never give up and I mean never." (F, https://www.universetoday.com/16440/elon-musk-i-will-never-give-up-after-falcon-1-loss/)
- **Day 4, public post "Timing is Everything."** A root cause from "Four methods of analysis," plus his own question "why didn't we catch this issue?" There is no emotional language (E).
- **Money.** A $20M Founders Fund investment was announced in the same week. He put his remaining money into both companies. By his own account (tweet, Nov 3, 2020) the Tesla round closed at 6pm on Christmas Eve, and he "had to borrow money from friends to pay rent" (V2 row 71). NASA's $1.6B cargo contract came on Dec 23, 2008. He said "I love you guys" on the call (60 Minutes 2014).
- **Later framing.** "That was definitely the worst year of my life." He volunteered the divorce as an extra blow, and added "This is the closest I've ever come" to a nervous breakdown (60 Minutes 2014, D).
- **Pattern.** Owns it, root cause fast, accelerates the next attempt, refuses to triage between companies. Emotion stays internal; public text is technical.

### 2. 2012: 60 Minutes, his heroes criticize him
- Asked about Armstrong's and Cernan's skepticism, he teared up: "Those guys are heroes of mine, so it's really tough." CBS's editor's note confirmed the tears (V2 row 39).
- **Pattern.** Criticism from people he admires produces hurt, not counterattack.

### 3. 2013: Model S fires and the NYT test drive
- **Fires.** His blog post gave the impact physics, the base rate ("5 times more likely" to have a fire in a gasoline car), and a categorical claim ("absolutely zero doubt"). He also took actions that put money behind the claim: a fire-warranty extension, an over-the-air ride-height change, and a request that NHTSA investigate (E, "Model S Fire", "The Mission of Tesla").
- **Broder (NYT).** He published the car's data logs, mocked the reporter with scare quotes, and quoted the reporter's own earlier article back at him. The apology went to the cause, not to the journalist: "We were played for a fool… For that, I am deeply sorry." (E)
- **Pattern.** His written crisis template (§2A) was already complete in 2013.

### 4. 2017–2018: Model 3 "production hell"
- He warned of "production hell" at first deliveries on July 28, 2017 (V2 row 13). By April 2018 he was sleeping at the factory.
- **Admission (X, Apr 13, 2018):** "Yes, excessive automation at Tesla was a mistake. To be precise, my mistake. Humans are underrated." (V2 row 1)
- **Workaround.** The GA4 line was built under a tent: "A new building was impossible, so we built a giant tent in 2 weeks" (X, June 19, 2018; Bloomberg said ~3 weeks). It built ~20% of the 5,031 Model 3s made in the last week of Q2 2018 (V2 rows 16–17).
- **Later nuance (TED 2022).** He rejected the simple automation story ("if we'd just done more manual stuff, things would have been fine. Of course not.") and said they "messed up almost every aspect" of the line (C).
- **Pattern.** Relocates to the bottleneck, makes it visible ("I slept on the floor, so the team… could see me"; TED 2022), admits the technical mistake, improvises.

### 5. May–Aug 2018: "Boring, bonehead questions" and the apology call
- **Q1 call, May 2, 2018.** He cut off the two most threatening questions on a cash-burn call, Bernstein's on capital needs ("Boring, bonehead questions are not cool. Next?") and RBC's on reservations, and told a Baird analyst asking for production updates "Please sell our stock and don't buy it." Most of the 75-minute call was patient (G).
- **Q2 call, Aug 1, 2018.** The first words of Q&A were an unprompted apology: "I'd like to apologize for being impolite on the prior call," "no excuse for bad manners." He gave 110–120-hour weeks as a reason, then said "there's still no excuse." He apologized to a second analyst by name. He made no apology to the analyst he'd told to sell (G).
- **Pattern.** Contempt toward the most threatening questions, then a clean apology once the cost (credibility with investors) was clear.

### 6. July 2018–2019: Thai cave rescue and "pedo guy"
- **Timeline (V2 row 12, H).**
  - Jul 15: the tweet.
  - Jul 18: apology: "his actions against me do not justify my actions against him, and for that I apologize."
  - Aug 28: re-escalation: "You don't think it's strange he hasn't sued me?"
  - Aug 30: an email to BuzzFeed headed "off the record," which BuzzFeed had not agreed to. It included "I fucking hope he sues me."
- **At trial (Dec 3–6, 2019).** Reciprocity logic: "I was upset. It was wrong and insulting and so I insulted him back"; "Admittedly, this is not classy." The jury found for him. His reaction: "My faith in humanity is restored" (F).
- **Still fighting in 2023:** "Also, that guy was not a cave diver." (Lex #400, A)
- **Pattern.** A sincere good deed, mocked, is his highest-risk trigger. A forced apology does not last. He litigates to the end rather than settle.

### 7. Aug 2018–2023: "Funding secured" and the SEC
- **Aug 7, 2018 tweet.** "Am considering taking Tesla private at $420. Funding secured." 17 days later came "Staying Public," which withdrew the plan **while restating** "more than enough funding" (E).
- **SEC.** He rejected an earlier offer ($10M fine, 2 years out as chair). The SEC sued on Sept 27. He settled on Sept 29 ($20M each from him and Tesla; out as chairman for 3 years). A Mark Cuban call reportedly helped (V2 row 35). Five days later he tweeted that the "Shortseller Enrichment Commission is doing incredible work" (Oct 4, 2018; V2 row 4).
- **The duress frame, stable for years:**
  - 60 Minutes 2018: "I do not respect the SEC," yet he abides by the settlement "Because I respect the justice system."
  - Tornetta trial 2022: settled "under duress" because "lenders put a gun to [his] head."
  - TED 2022: "gun to your child's head… Those bastards"; "I was forced to admit that I lied to save Tesla's life."
  - Lex #400 2023: "I did actually have the funding secured"; "This is like a hostage negotiation"; SEC lawyers want a "trophy" for "their LinkedIn."
- **Outcome.** A 2023 jury cleared him in the investor class action. There he argued from intent ("I had no ill motive") and from track record (F). The SEC rebuffed his 2023 attempt to undo the decree [reported, CNBC 2023-02-23].
- **Pattern.** Settles under duress, never concedes in his own mind, and turns the settlement into a story of coercion. A classic integrity fight (Part 3, D).

### 8. Aug 2018: The NYT "excruciating" interview
- He choked up over missing his birthday and his brother's wedding ("All night — no friends, nothing"). Asked whether he regretted the tweet: "Why would I?" He also denied getting calls from the board, which Tesla later corrected (D, [reported] NYT).
- Afterward he disputed the framing: his voice "cracked once," there were "no tears" [contested: NYT stood by its account].
- **Pattern.** Defiance and vulnerability in one sitting. Emotion is triggered by family sacrifice. He fights how his emotion is described.

### 9. 2018–2022: Short sellers
- "a box of short shorts" for Einhorn (X, Aug 1, 2018); "Only $69.420!!" short shorts (X, July 5, 2020; V2 row 37); "They wanted Tesla to die so bad, they could taste it" (TED 2022). **Pattern:** moral enemies; wins are mocked with merchandise.

### 10. Nov 2019: Cybertruck window smash
- On stage: a curse, then "room for improvement" (V2 row 52). Next day: a cause (an earlier sledgehammer hit) and an order count. Later: "At least you know that our demos are authentic." (Rogan #1470) **Pattern:** humor, then cause, then a number; failure becomes marketing.

### 11. 2020: COVID and Alameda County
- He tweeted "FREE AMERICA NOW" (Apr 28). On the Apr 29 call he called the orders "fascist" (V2 row 36). He sued the county (May 9) and threatened to move HQ and programs to Texas or Nevada. On May 11: "Tesla is restarting production today against Alameda County rules… If anyone is arrested, I ask that it only be me." (H, PolitiTweet id 1259945593805221891). The county backed down. HQ moved to Austin in 2021.
- **Miss.** "probably close to zero new cases in US too by end of April" (X, Mar 19, 2020; V2).
- **Pattern.** He escalates on several fronts at once (sue, threaten to leave, defy, volunteer as martyr) and follows through on the structural threat.

### 12. Nov 2021: Raptor "production crisis" email
- "There is no way to sugarcoat this." Problems were "far more severe than was reported" after "the exiting of prior senior management." He said: "I will be on the Raptor line all night and through the weekend," and "we face a genuine risk of bankruptcy" without a Starship flight every two weeks (F).
- **Pattern.** Internally, bad news is stated flat, with a causal chain to survival and the leader at the line in person. He partly blames prior management for under-reporting.

### 13. 2022: Twitter, from courtship to forced close
- **Apr 5–7.** He was self-effacing in texts ("lmk if I'm pushing too hard"; "calibrate the dumbness of my suggestions").
- **Apr 9.** One pushback from CEO Agrawal triggered a switch within one minute: "What did you get done this week?" → "I'm not joining the board. This is a waste of time." → "Will make an offer to take Twitter private." (F, court exhibit)
- **May 8.** Two weeks after signing he texted "Let's slow down just a few days," citing Putin and WW3, and set up a bot-count "showstopper" in writing (F).
- **July.** He tried to terminate. Twitter sued in Delaware. In early October he reversed and offered to close at the original price.
- **Oct 26–28.** "Entering Twitter HQ – let that sink in!" and "Comedy is now legal on Twitter." On Nov 16 came "A Fork in the Road": "we will need to be extremely hardcore" (V2 row 10).
- **Admission (BBC, Apr 2023).** He closed because a court would have forced him: "yes that is the reason" (D).
- **Self-correction posts.** "My apologies. Won't happen again." after the rival-links ban (Dec 18, 2022); after losing his own poll on stepping down, he would resign "as soon as I find someone foolish enough to take the job!" (Dec 21, 2022; V2).
- **Pattern.** When cornered he pivots to the inevitable outcome without admitting defeat, and turns the forced close into a joke he controls. Policy reversals come with a short operational apology.

### 14. Oct 2022–2024: Advertisers, Media Matters, GARM
- **Courtship.** "Dear Twitter Advertisers" (Oct 27, 2022) was his warmest register: "I did it to try to help humanity, whom I love." (E)
- **Nov 2023.** He replied "You have said the actual truth" to an antisemitic post. A Media Matters report followed and advertisers paused. On Nov 18: "X Corp will be filing a thermonuclear lawsuit" (H).
- **DealBook, Nov 29, 2023** (Rev transcript, D):
  - The apology: "I handed a loaded gun to those who hate me… For that, I'm quite sorry"; "the worst and dumbest post that I've ever done."
  - Within about 2 minutes: "go fuck yourself… Hey, Bob, if you're in the audience," then "GFY." The boycott would "kill the company" and "we'll document it in great detail."
  - "The judge is the public." His Israel trip "wasn't some apology tour."
- **Aug 6, 2024.** X sued WFA/GARM and several advertisers. GARM shut down Aug 9. Fidelity had marked X down ~71.5% (V2 row 34).
- **Pattern.** Courtship, then a perceived betrayal or boycott, then open hostility. He apologizes for the content and goes to war with the reaction.

### 15. Jan 2024: The WSJ drug report and the Lemon interview
- **WSJ (Jan 6, 2024)** reported illegal drug use. His reply on X the next day: about 3 years of random testing at NASA's request found "not even trace quantities," and "If drugs actually helped improve my net productivity over time, I would definitely take them!" (V2 row 24)
- **Don Lemon (Mar 2024)** [reported]. On ketamine he answered under protest ("pretty private to ask"), then: "From the standpoint of Wall Street, what matters is execution." On moderation: "I don't have to answer questions from reporters, Don." Within hours he canceled the show deal and posted that Lemon "lacked authenticity" (D).
- **Pattern.** Allegations about private conduct get a denial plus results as the alibi ("execution"). Retaliation comes afterward, on X.

### 16. Jan 2024–Dec 2025: Delaware voids the pay package
- **Jan 30, 2024.** Same day as the ruling: "Never incorporate your company in the state of Delaware." SpaceX's move to Texas was announced Feb 14. Neuralink moved to Nevada. Tesla moved to Texas after the June 13, 2024 vote, which also re-ratified the package (V2 row 73). Personal attacks on Chancellor McCormick by name [unverified].
- **Dec 19, 2025.** The Delaware Supreme Court restored the package. It reversed the rescission remedy and awarded $1 nominal damages; the award was worth ~$139B at that day's close. Musk posted that he was "vindicated" (V1 row 17).
- **Pattern.** Attacks the institution, exits the jurisdiction, re-runs the vote, appeals. A win gets one short post.

### 17. 2023–2026: Starship tests
- **Flight 1 (Apr 20, 2023).** He set the bar low in advance (clearing the pad). Afterward: "Congrats @SpaceX team on an exciting test launch of Starship! Learned a lot for next test launch in a few months." (Tier 1)
- **2025.** Flights 7, 8 and 9 (Jan 16, Mar 6, May 27) lost three ships in a row; Flights 10 and 11 (Aug 26, Oct 13) succeeded (V1 row 67). The night of Flight 7 he posted a preliminary cause ("oxygen/fuel leak in the cavity above the ship engine firewall") and "Success is uncertain, but entertainment is guaranteed!" with debris video (V1 row 69).
- **Ship 36 (June 18, 2025).** It exploded on the stand. Within hours he posted a preliminary cause (a nitrogen COPV that failed below proof pressure) (V1 row 68). A "Just a scratch" post that day is [unverified].
- **2026.** Flight 12 (May 22, the V3 debut) followed a scrub the day before; the booster failed its boostback burn. Flight 14 (Sep 28) reached orbit, but an engine failure cut it short (V1 rows 7–8). On the SpaceX Q2 2026 call: rockets "desperately want to blow themselves into tiny pieces," and a jab ("some of our competitors do, in fact, blow their rockets up") followed at once by a courtesy (G).

## Part 2. Mechanics

### 2A. His written crisis playbook (E: Falcon 1 2008, Model S fire and recall 2013, Broder 2013)
1. **Publish the physics or root cause fast.** Four days after Falcon 1 Flight 3; same evening for Starship 2025.
2. **Compare to the baseline.** Gas-car fire rates; "disproportionate by several orders of magnitude" media coverage.
3. **Make a categorical claim** ("absolutely zero doubt").
4. **Back the claim with money or action.** A warranty extension, logged data, an over-the-air fix, an invitation to the regulator. A recall is framed as voluntary: "No regulatory agency brought this to our attention."
5. **Close with civilizational stakes** (delaying sustainable transport "cannot be allowed to happen").
6. **Emotion stays internal.** Internal email gets "come hell or high water"; the public post is technical.
7. **Toward hostile press:** a data dump, the reporter's own past words quoted, scare quotes, a formal demand for correction. No benefit of the doubt once facts conflict.

**Counter-example.** On X after 2023, the data dump often collapses into the generic line "Legacy media lies" (H §3.6).

### 2B. How he fights on X (H, measured)
- **Burst escalation.** About 40 posts in under 3 hours on June 5, 2025 (17:39–20:26 UTC). The mechanics:
  - quote-posting the opponent's own past words ("Where is the Mike Johnson of 2023!?")
  - short labeling verdicts ("criminal," "hypocrite," "lie," "bs," "propaganda")
  - ally amplification ("💯," "Exactly")
  - laugh emoji on attacks ("🤣🤣")
  - a closing irony line ("Most entertaining outcome is most likely")
- **Denial formula.** "This is false" / "categorically false" / "Utterly false" / "Headline is misleading," followed by blaming the outlet. Before 2022 he named the outlet and gave counter-numbers (FT on Starlink, 2022). After 2023 it is the generic "Legacy media lies," sometimes with a Grok link as proof.
- **Escalate, clarify, ennoble.** Profanity and threats first, then a narrowed target, then a patriotic restatement (H-1B, Dec 2024; E).
- **Superlative legal threats.** "thermonuclear lawsuit" (2023).
- **Apologies are operational.** "Sorry" plus a technical cause plus the fix ("Sorry about that. Fixing as fast as possible!", 2022; "Apologies, we will take measures…", 2021). Personal apologies are rare, come after private contact, and shift part of the blame (Halli 2023; Trump 2025 after a call). Sarcastic pseudo-apologies are about as common: "I would like to apologize for firing these geniuses" (Nov 15, 2022).
- **Timing.** Heavy posting midnight–3 am Central. His worst self-inflicted posts were impulsive ("pedo guy," "funding secured," the "actual truth" reply, the Epstein post). His joking rule: "I should not tweet uh after 3am" (BBC 2023). Advisers urged a Twitter break in 2018 [reported, Bloomberg via BNN 2019].
- **Volume tracks political fights.** About 150 posts/day in Feb–Mar 2025, about 48–52/day during the feud period, and about 45–55/day by mid-2026 (H §2.1).

**The five highest-value moves for prediction:**
1. **Stupid, not malicious.** Asked whether he lied or acted in bad faith, he readily admits being dumb, impulsive, late or "a huge idiot" in order to defeat the worse charge (Stahl 2018, Faber 2023, DealBook 2023, BBC 2023: 4 interviews).
2. **Apologize for content, attack the reaction.** "quite sorry," then "go fuck yourself" within about two minutes (DealBook 2023). Same structure: Broder 2013, Unsworth 2018, Trump 2025.
3. **Civil in the room, retaliation on X 0–5 days later** (Lemon 2024, Pogue 2025, Economist 2026: 3 cases). The 2018 version: the "pedo guy" re-escalation came weeks after the apology.
4. **Emotional triggers are family, heroes and employees,** never personal insults. Insults get "Hate away" or "I don't care" (DealBook 2023, Economist 2026).
5. **Since 2025: topic fencing and the "NPC" label for repeated questions** (Husain 2025 and Economist 2026, both secondary sources; Pogue 2025). Before 2023 he engaged on almost any topic.

**Counter-examples.** Friendly rooms get deference (26% of the words on Hannity 2025, many one-word agreements). Long, low-key formats get more concessions (Swisher 2018: "self-inflicted wounds," and "I'm sorry to some journalists" once she offered the chance). Technical or business interviewers get patience (Faber 2023; Pelley 2014).

### 2D. Courtroom moves (F: SolarCity 2021, Tornetta 2022, funding secured 2023, Musk v. Altman 2026)
- **Calls the question a trick, in near-identical words across four trials:**
  - "Your questions are so deceptive it's silly" (2021)
  - "Your question is a complex question that is commonly used to mislead people" (2022)
  - "Your questions are not simple, they are designed to trick me, essentially" and "It is a lie to say they are simple" (2026)
- **Separates the court from the lawyer.** "I think you are a bad human being… I have great respect for the court, but not for you, sir." (2021) The same split as 60 Minutes 2018 (SEC vs justice system).
- **Concedes facts minimally.** "Maybe," "partly," "it's possible," "Possibly. It's not out of the question."
- **Repeats a one-line moral thesis** until it is struck ("steal a charity," 2026).
- **Humor as a pressure valve.** "Master of Coin," "law 101," "some karma around 420."
- **Argues intent and track record.** "I had no ill motive"; "I've done a very good job for investors" (2023).
- **What judges found.** His testimony on effort and pain was credible ("totally credible," Tornetta). His process memory was repeatedly unreliable: claims with "no support in the contemporaneous record," and an account called "implausible." In SolarCity the judge found "no threats, fits or fights" toward the board.
- **Pattern.** Fights the frame, not the facts: self-crediting on direct, narrowing on cross.

### 2E. Private exit when trust breaks (F)
- **OpenAI 2017.** A challenge to his motives and control led within minutes to "Discussions are over" and, under an hour later, "That is no longer on the table." Funding became leverage: "I will no longer fund OpenAI until…" The first "I've had enough" email went out at 12:08 AM.
- **Twitter 2022.** Deference ("lmk if I'm pushing too hard"), then a cliff: one complaint from Agrawal, and inside one minute he dropped the board seat and announced a takeover offer.
- **Rule.** He doesn't schedule the hard conversation. He removes the old deal and changes the game: funds it himself, buys it, or competes (xAI). Contrast this with his own claim: "To be frank, I've almost never been betrayed." (Lex #400, 2023)

### 2F. Bad quarters and missed targets (G: 18 Tesla calls 2018–2026, plus SpaceX Q2 2026)
1. **The CFO gives the numbers; he gives the thesis.** In every weak quarter (Q1 2024, Q4 2024, Q1 2025, Q2 2025, Q4 2025), CFO Vaibhav Taneja explains the declines while Musk talks autonomy and Optimus.
2. **Pre-empts in the opening with a reframe:**
   - "I want to put that concern to rest" (Q4 2022, on demand)
   - "between two major growth waves" (Q4 2023)
   - "not on the ragged edge of death" (Q1 2025)
3. **Blames macro, not the brand.** Interest rates, "crystal ball" (×5 in one 2023 call), "not immune to the macro demand for cars." He never concedes brand damage from his politics.
4. **Admits optimism as a category, never item by item, then gives a new date:**
   - "boy who cried FSD" (Q2 2023)
   - "overly optimistic in the past" → "I would be shocked if we cannot do it next year" (Q2 2024)
   - "there's a damn wolf this time"; "the media reports on when I'm late but never reports when I'm early" (Q4 2024)
   - "We are not always on time, but we get it done" (Q2 2025)
   - In ten 2024–26 calls he never once said a specific target was missed.
5. **Silent re-scoping.** Robotaxi coverage went from half the US population, to metros, to states, to weekly miles. Optimus went from "thousands" in factories to "still in the R&D phase."
6. **After 2025 misses: S-curve fatalism.** "impossible to predict" (Q1 2026); "flat and long" (Q2 2026). The far-off mega-numbers stay.
7. **Curt rather than hostile since 2018.** "what's your follow-up?"; "Um, no."; "inject some reality into these questions" (Q1 2026). Doubters of autonomy are told, in effect, to sell (Q1–Q2 2024).
8. **Deliberate padding.** He planned 20 GW and expects ~15 because "maybe 1/4 of the projects take longer than expected" (SpaceX Q2 2026).
9. **Counter-example.** The SpaceX Q2 2026 call had no hostility at all. He offered analysts plug numbers and showed regulatory caution ("we don't want to jump the gun on regulators").

### 2G. Emotional tells and internal state
- **Choke-up triggers are consistent** (D, 5 cases): heroes turning on him (Pelley 2012), missed family time (NYT 2018), harm to his companies' people (Kudlow, Baier 2025), his estranged child (Peterson 2024). Never a technical failure or a personal insult.

"it's not like I you know have a stone cold heart" (BBC 2023). Yet he disputes reports of tears (NYT 2018) and redirects "this hurt you personally" to "it hurt the company, too" (DealBook 2023).
- **Coping by reattribution.** "the attacks are by people that actually don't know me and they're trying to generate clicks" (Lex #438). "if we're the target, we're doing something right" (Hannity 2025).
- **Stress registers as relief.** "not so much elation as relief" (Lex #252, Tier 1).

## Part 3. Reaction playbook (by trigger)

Confidence: **High** = 5+ consistent episodes; **Med** = 3–4 with exceptions; **Low** = thin or mixed.

### A. Public criticism of him or his companies
- **Default.** A fast reply on X: a one-word verdict, a meme, a laugh emoji, or a motive reframe ("short sellers," "legacy media lies," "paid for," "they want censorship"). No substance in the first hour.
- **When he engages the substance:** the critic is a respected builder or hero (Pelley 2012), or the issue is technical and fixable (Cybertruck, Starship, Model S fire). Then he answers with data, logs or video.
- **Prediction rules**
  - Generalized criticism ("your platform is more hateful") → he demands one specific example, then calls the critic a liar if none comes. **Med** (BBC 2023; Rogan #2223 and #2281 "show me where I am wrong": 3).
  - Criticism with a plausible financial or ideological motive behind it → he names the motive before the facts. **High** (UAW 2018, short sellers 2018, Tesla protests 2025, Media Matters 2023: 6+).
  - A hostile interviewer → civil in the room, harsh on X within 0–5 days. **Med-High** (3 cases).

### B. A failure he caused
- **Engineering failure → owns it, root cause, next date, upbeat.** **High** (Falcon 1, Model 3 automation, Cybertruck, Starship ×6, Raptor: 8+). "if there's something that goes wrong with that vehicle, it's fundamentally my fault" (Lex #252).
- **A post or statement that blows up → defend first; apologize narrowly only if the cost is concrete,** meaning a stock or revenue hit, a counterparty with power, or a courtroom. **High** (bonehead 2018, Unsworth 2018, DealBook 2023, Trump 2025: 4 clear cases, plus Twitter policy 2022). The apology covers words or tone ("went too far," "dumbest post," "no excuse for bad manners"). The position underneath stays, and he often re-escalates later (Unsworth: 6 weeks).
- **A missed schedule → concedes the category, enforces his own hedge, gives a new date.** **High** (§2F; 15+ calls).
- **Almost never apologizes for:** political views, statements about family, attacks on institutions (SEC, Delaware, media, proxy advisors), the substance of decisions (layoffs, DOGE cuts), or anything framed as free speech.
- **Counter-example.** Unprompted, specific apologies happen when the matter is small and the person was harmed by his factual error, after private contact (Halli 2023), or when the audience is investors he needs (Q2 2018).

### C. A failure outside his control (macro, pandemic, supplier, regulator delay)
- **Default.** Names the external constraint loudly ("fascist," interest rates, "stormy weather," tariffs "entirely up to the President"), then works around it physically: tent line, reopening the factory, moving HQ, insourcing.
- **Prediction rule.** Weak demand → "macro," never brand. **High** (Q4 2022–Q1 2025: 6 calls).

### D. An attack on his integrity (a lie, malice, fraud, bigotry)
- **Default.** A detailed counterattack that can last years. It cites verdicts, documents and incentives, and is paired with a cheap self-insult about competence.
- **Prediction rules**
  - "You lied" → "I'm dumb at dates, not dishonest," often with Hanlon's razor. **High** (Stahl 2018, DealBook 2023, Lex #400, funding-secured trial: 4+).
  - "You're antisemitic / racist / a Nazi" → "Did you read the whole thing?", a single counterexample, "the normal play book." **Med** (DealBook 2023, Baier 2025, Economist 2026, Welt 2024).
  - Old integrity charges never close. He re-argues funding secured and the cave diver in 2022–23. **High.**
- **The contrast that predicts tone.** He "concedes on schedule but fights on integrity" (A, Lex #400): in one sitting he volunteered "far too optimistic about autopilot" and fought "funding secured," the SEC and the cave diver.

### E. Legal or regulatory threat
- **Default sequence:**
  1. Contempt for the agency or judge ("I do not respect the SEC," "Never incorporate… in Delaware," "corporate terrorists").
  2. Sue or countersue, often in a friendly venue (Texas for Media Matters and GARM).
  3. Threaten to exit, and do it (Alameda → Austin; Delaware → Texas and Nevada).
  4. If it becomes existential, settle or capitulate tactically, then describe it as duress (SEC 2018, Twitter close 2022).
  5. A win gets one short post ("Vindicated," "faith in humanity is restored").
  6. A loss is procedural ("a calendar technicality") and he vows to appeal.
- **Confidence: High** (SEC, Alameda, Twitter, Media Matters/GARM, Delaware, OpenAI, proxy advisors: 7).
- **On the stand.** "Your questions are designed to trick me," minimal concessions, a repeated moral one-liner, humor. **High** (4 trials).
- **Counter-example.** Toward courts as institutions he is formally respectful ("I respect the justice system"). As a public company CEO in 2026 he is visibly cautious with regulators (SpaceX Q2 2026).

### F. Betrayal by an ally, co-founder or someone he helped
- **Default.** Immediate exit, the old offer withdrawn, a rival built or bought, moral framing ("steal a charity," "ingratitude," "bait and switch"), litigation and a grudge lasting years. **High** (OpenAI 2017–26, Twitter board 2022, advertisers 2023, Gates 2022–25).
- **When he backs down:** only when the other side holds asymmetric power over his companies and there is private contact first. Then it takes about a week, the regret is about tone, and the fight moves to policy (Trump, June 2025). **Med**, mostly one data point.
- **Counter-example.** He said in 2023 he wanted to be friends with Larry Page again (Lex #400).

### G. Financial danger
- **Default.** Doubles his personal bet and refuses to triage. Moves in physically ("I will be on the Raptor line all night"). Talks survival math internally ("genuine risk of bankruptcy," "120 days you're dead"). Cuts hard (Twitter, 2022), and tells the public it's fine ("not on the ragged edge of death").
- **His word for it:** triage: "So in times of crisis, allocation changes to where the crisis is." (Tornetta 2022, Tier 1)
- **Confidence: High** (2008, 2017–18, 2021, 2022, 2025).

### H. Being mocked or ridiculed
- **Default.** Out-trolls the mocker with a joke that takes over the narrative (the sink, short shorts, "Just a scratch," "Success is uncertain, but entertainment is guaranteed!"). **High.**
- **Danger case.** If the mockery targets something sincere (the cave sub), it can spiral into a reckless personal attack. **Med** (one major case, but high-cost).

### I. Praise or a win
- **Default.** Short thanks, credit to "the team" with "!!", then immediately a bigger goal. A courtroom or legal win gets one word. **Med-High** (H §3.2; Starship, robotaxi launch 2025, Delaware 2025, pay vote 2025).
- Establishment praise does not soften him toward the establishment.

### J. A hostile interview or press inquiry (his own seat)
- **Rules, in order of reliability:**
  1. A loaded or sentimental premise → he rejects or redefines it first ("Have I?", "Promise is a strong word"). **High** (A: all 5 Lex episodes).
  2. Repeated questions → "you're not hearing me" (2018), now "NPC" (2025–26). **Med-High.**
  3. Off-topic policy questions since 2025 → fences the topic. **Med** (2–3 cases).
  4. Private conduct → answers under protest, then "what matters is execution." **Med.**
  5. Press inquiries to X → 💩 auto-reply (Mar 2023) [reported, NPR].

## Part 4. How his reactions changed over time (what's true now)

| Era | Dominant crisis style | Key evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **2008–2014** | Owns failure, writes data-rich rebuttals, modest ("we've been fortunate"), visibly hurt by heroes | Falcon 1 emails; 2013 blogs; 60 Minutes 2012, 2014 |
| **2017–2019** | Impulsive posts create the crises (pedo guy, funding secured, bonehead); apologies are narrow and sometimes reversed; the SEC war begins | Cases 4–8 |
| **2020–2022** | Defiance of authority (Alameda), meme dominance, Twitter takeover; operational apologies on X | Cases 10–13 |
| **2023–2024** | X owner: generic "legacy media lies," litigation as first resort (Media Matters, GARM), Delaware exit, GFY to advertisers | Cases 14–16 |
| **2025** | Peak combativeness (~150 posts/day in DOGE's early months); persecution frame; the Trump feud and a narrow regret; "NPC" and topic fencing appear | Cases 19–22 |
| **2026 (now)** | Lower volume (~45–55/day), AI-focused; court losses recast as procedural (OpenAI); post-interview retaliation persists (Economist); as a public-company CEO (SpaceX) he is friendlier with analysts and cautious with regulators; S-curve fatalism replaces dated unit targets | Cases 23, 26; §2F |

**Stable across all eras.** Physics failures get calm and humor. Integrity charges get a long fight. Apologies cover words, not decisions. He goes to the bottleneck in person. Escalation comes before refinement.

**Changed.** Denials went from specific to generic; topic fencing is new (2025); partisan targets shifted; self-description softened ("more chill than I used to be… I'm not that laid back", Dimon 2026); an Economist clip titled "I got too involved in politics" [reported; his exact words not confirmed] (2026) sits beside his defense of every position.

## Part 5. Cross-cutting rules for the skill

1. **Classify the trigger first:** physics, schedule, integrity, institution, betrayal, or family. Each gets a different Musk.
2. **He trades a cheap concession for an expensive denial.** "Dumb, late, impulsive, huge idiot" protects "honest, well-intentioned."
3. **An apology is narrow, cost-triggered** (stock hit, powerful counterparty, courtroom, private contact) **and often followed by attack** on the people who reacted.
4. **He moves toward the fire.** Factory floor, Raptor line, Twitter HQ, data center. Visible shared pain is deliberate.
5. **He goes public before the press,** usually within hours and often after midnight. Legacy media are bypassed.
6. **He escalates on several fronts at once,** and follows through on structural threats (moving HQ, reincorporating, building a rival). Performative threats (cage match, America Party) often lapse.
7. **The existential frame justifies everything** ("ship will sink," "kill the company," "robot army").
8. **Humor is a control device** in the first hour of an engineering or ego failure, rarely when people are hurt.
9. **Grudges are kept and scored** (SEC 2018→2023, Unsworth 2018→2023, Gates 2022→2025, OpenAI 2017→2026).
10. **Words and company actions diverge.** Loud public defense, quiet internal fix (Grok, DOGE time, analyst apology).

**Quick template.** Fast sharp or joking reply → reframe as mission, free speech or survival → move into the problem with a deadline → if an institution pushes, sue or relocate → concede only tone or schedule, only at concrete cost → bring it up again years later.
