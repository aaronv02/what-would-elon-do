## §0. The model in one paragraph

Musk manages as a chief engineer with near-total authority who says he dislikes managing. His loop, documented in his own emails and testimony from 2008 to 2026:

1. Frame survival: flat bad news, often a bankruptcy chain.
2. Set a numeric target with a hard date.
3. Make himself the approval gate.
4. Default-deny: anything stays only if a named person vouches for it.
5. Go to the bottleneck in person.
6. Cut deep, accept collateral damage, rehire some.
7. Credit "the team" in public.

Privately he self-deprecates and asks to be told when he's wrong. That humility ends abruptly when someone challenges his standing or motives. His own triage rule, under oath in 2022: "So in times of crisis, allocation changes to where the crisis is." [R] **T1**

**Now (2025–26):** the loop is unchanged. What has shifted:
- **Where cuts land:** in the unit he judges behind (xAI). Tesla says "no layoff plans" while scaling Optimus.
- **His attention:** split, and he concedes the DOGE detour was costly.
- **His public self-presentation:** softer ("more chill," hires need "a good heart," 2026).

## §2. Core patterns

### P1. How he writes orders and emails

**Template** [verified, F: full texts of SpaceX 2008/2010/2021, Tesla Apr 2018/May 2018/May 2022 ×2, Twitter Nov 9 and Nov 16, 2022]:

| Step | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Open | Praise with exact numbers, or bad news stated flat | "There is no way to sugarcoat this." [O] **T1** (Raptor 2021; near-identical opener in Twitter, Nov 9, 2022) |
| 2. Target | Quantified, with a hard date | "850 sets of car parts in 24 hours no later than June 30th" [O]; Twitter's ~40-hour ultimatum |
| 3. Why | The causal or margin chain | Raptor → Starship → Starlink V2 → bankruptcy |
| 4. Gate | Himself as the escalation or exception path | "present that to me directly"; remote exceptions "I will review and approve… directly" [O] |
| 5. Rules | Short bullets | Meetings, acronyms, chain of command (2018) |
| 6. Close | Thanks, often a joke | "Thanks for being such a kickass team and accomplishing miracles every day. It matters." [O] (2018) |

**Subject lines are verdicts:** "A Fork in the Road," "To be super clear," "Acronyms Seriously Suck," "Remote work is no longer acceptble" [sic].

**Short yes, long no** [verified, F: ~40 OpenAI emails and 40 pages of 2022 texts]:
- **Agreement takes one to four words:** "Agree on all" (his whole reply to Altman's governance proposal, 2015), "Sounds good," "Sure," "Ok."
- **Paragraphs appear only** for disagreement, orders or big asks.
- **Frustration shows up as exit, not argument:** "Guys, I've had enough… Discussions are over" (OpenAI, Sept 2017), then "That is no longer on the table." [O]

**Sign-offs and tics:**
- "Thanks, Elon" on formal texts; "Elon" on longer emails; nothing on short ones; "--Elon--" in 2008.
- Words: "Frankly," "To be clear," "super," "~," "lmk."
- Timing: 12–4 AM timestamps.

**Two registers:** courteous and logistical with executives and dealmakers; profane with friends ("dog shit features").

**Escalation:** a same-day follow-up widens the scope. The 2022 remote-work order went from execs to everyone, adding "If you don't show up, we will assume you have resigned." [O] **T1**

**Prediction rules (P1)**
- **A sound proposal:** a two-to-four-word approval, immediately. *High (2 corpora, 2015–22).*
- **An order:** numbers or flat bad news → target plus date → why → "come to me" → thanks. *High (8+ emails).*
- **Continued defiance:** a sharper second email the same day, with wider scope. *Medium-high (2022; 2010 "drastic action… I have given enough warnings").*
- **Reading his tone:** courtesy ("Whatever decision you make, thank you for your efforts" [O]) often wraps a maximally forcing mechanism. Don't read it as softness.

### P2. Default-deny with a named owner; the "excellent, necessary and trustworthy" test

**The rule:** anything stays only if a named person stakes their reputation on it. The burden of proof is on whoever wants to keep it.

| Year | Instance | Label |
|---|---|---|
| 2010 | "Unless an acronym is approved by me, it should not enter the SpaceX glossary." [O] | [verified] |
| Apr 2018 | Spend over $1M "on hold until explicitly approved by me" [O]. Supplier quotes need "a detailed, first principles understanding… every line item" [O]. A rule fit for "a great Dilbert cartoon" should change [O] | [verified] |
| May 2018 | Each contractor justified "by individual" for "excellence, necessity and trustworthiness" [O]. "By default, anyone who does not have a Tesla employee putting their reputation on the line for them will be denied access" [O] | [verified] |
| Apr 29, 2024 | Resignation sought from any exec "who retains more than three people who don't obviously pass the excellent, necessary and trustworthy test" [R] | [reported, The Information] |
| 2024 | "The requirements are always dumb to some degree." [O] **T1** "If you're not forced to put back at least 10% of what you delete, you're not deleting enough." [O] **T1** He warns teams in advance that some deletions will return | [verified, Lex #438] |
| 2025 | DOGE payments need "some explanation… above nothing" [A]; for aid, "Send me a picture of the thing." [A] | [verified, JRE #2281] |

**The naming rule's source:** the requirement that each requirement carry a person's name, not a department, is Isaacson's account [reported]. Lex #438 says requirements from even smart people are dumb, but doesn't state the naming rule.

**Gate placement:** in nearly every case the gate is Musk himself. That covers acronyms, $1M spend, remote exceptions, Twitter office exceptions and the 2022 hiring pause. Centralizing is the mechanism, which sits oddly with his stated dislike of being boss (P12).

**Prediction rules (P2)**
- **Something whose need nobody can show:** removed by default; the owner can argue it back. *High (6 instances, 2010–25).*
- **Headcount, contractor or vendor review:** per-person justification against excellent, necessary and trustworthy, with a deadline. *High for the method; medium for the exact words.*
- **Counter-condition:** he claims to comply with "10 million regulations" while disputing five (DealBook 2023). In the user's seat, laws, permits and safety rules are never deletable.

### P3. Hiring: drill-down, ability over credentials, uncapped spend on talent

**Ability over credentials** (stable 2014–2025):
- "There's no need even to have a college degree at all, or even high school." [R] **T1** (Auto Bild 2014; he looks for "evidence of exceptional ability")
- "Educational background is irrelevant" [P] **T1** (Feb 2020, with a "hardcore coding test" and a direct report line to him [reported, CNBC])
- OpenAI, Dec 2015: recruit people "who haven't completed their grad or even undergrad, but are obviously brilliant" [O] [verified]

**Engineers over managers:**
- "I interface way better with engineers who are able to do hardcore programming than with program manager / MBA types of people." [O] (to Agrawal, 2022)
- For Twitter: "Please send me anyone who actually writes good software"; "no CXO titles" [O] [verified, F]
- Wired 2012: "I don't believe in process." [O] **T1** A candidate who says it's "all about the process" is "a bad sign," because process "becomes a substitute for thinking" [O]

**Drill-down interview** [unverified as quoted]:
- **The method:** ask for the life story and the hardest problem solved, then probe level by level until a pretender "gets stuck."
- **Source:** a World Government Summit 2017 account whose transcript could not be reached (Tier 2).
- **Consistent with:** his 2022 wish to read the codebase to "calibrate the dumbness of my suggestions" [O], and the takeover practice of asking engineers to show their code. The Oct 2022 code-printout episode has conflicting accounts [unverified].

**Uncapped talent spend and personal recruiting** [verified, OpenAI email exhibits]:
- "We need to do what it takes to get the top talent. Let's go higher." [O] (2016)
- "Either we get the best people in the world or we will get whipped by Deepmind." [O] **T1**
- "The output of any company is the vector sum of the people within it." [O] **T1**
- Against DeepMind counteroffers: he would call wavering recruits "personally… this is my absolute top priority 24/7." [O] (2015)
- He interviewed almost every one of SpaceX's first ~1,000 hires [reported, Isaacson].
- **Talent capture as strategy:** on three Tesla calls in 2024 he said xAI exists because AI researchers "were not going to come to Tesla under any circumstances" [O]. A 2017 email surfaced at the 2026 trial about recruiting Karpathy: "The OpenAI guys are going to want to kill me." [R]

**Character, a rising emphasis:**
- **2013:** at SXSW his biggest mistake was over-weighting talent against personality and a "good heart" [reported, V2 row 47]. He later repeated it on a podcast.
- **2025:** YC: "a major failure mode is when ego to ability ratio is greater than one" [A].
- **2026:** Dimon interview: "it also matters if they have a good heart." [O]

**Loyalists imported in crises:**
- **Twitter 2022:** Tesla engineers and his cousins [reported, Isaacson].
- **DOGE 2025:** company people, e.g. Steve Davis [reported].
- **xAI, April 2026:** Starlink SVP Michael Nicolls installed as president. His memo targeted parity with Claude and called training efficiency "embarrassingly low" [verified, V1 row 43].

**Prediction rules (P3)**
- **Screening:** he ignores degrees, asks what the person personally built, and drills one problem to the bottom. *High for the stance (5+ sources); medium for the exact script.*
- **An exceptional person at the bottleneck:** pay is no object, he recruits personally, and moves fast. *High.*
- **Coordinator or manager candidates:** default skepticism. *Medium-high.*
- **In a crisis:** he imports insiders from his other companies rather than outsiders. *Medium-high (3 cases).*

   - Days earlier he had asked Agrawal to say if he was "pushing too hard."
2. **Public contradiction.**
   - **Nov 2022:** engineer Eric Frohnhoefer fired after a public thread disputing Musk's technical claims; internal-Slack critics also fired [unverified].

Musk on the stand: "I don't yell at people, basically," and "You occasionally have to use strong language to get people to change their course." [R]
3. **Resisting cuts.**
   - The 2024 exec email says "most" execs were "not yet" taking headcount seriously [reported].
4. **Missing the bar after a deadline:** "now time is up" (2018).

**Contested Vance (2015) anecdotes:**
- **Assistant Mary Beth Brown** (~12 years).
  - *Vance:* after she asked for a raise, Musk did her job for two weeks, then let her go.
  - *Musk* (tweets, Aug 10, 2017): "total nonsense"; the role "required several specialists vs one generalist"; she got "52 weeks of salary & stock." [contested, V2 row 20]

**Prediction rules (P7)**

- **Private, data-backed technical pushback:** tolerated, and it can change the decision. *Medium-high.*
- **A challenge to his motives:** an immediate switch from cooperation to a power move, with no call first. *High (OpenAI 2017–18, Twitter 2022).*

### P10. Motivation

**1. Survival plus an upbeat close.**
- Raptor bankruptcy chain (2021).
- "there is a good chance Twitter will not survive the upcoming economic downturn" [O], then "The potential is truly incredible!" [O] (Nov 9, 2022).
- "Unless this exercise is successful, the ship of America will sink." [O] (Baier, 2025).

**2. Intensity as the price of admission.**
- "There are way easier places to work, but nobody ever changed the world on 40 hours a week" [P] **T1**; "about 80 sustained, peaking above 100 at times" [R] **T1** (2018).
- "DOGE is working 120 hour a week. Our bureaucratic opponents optimistically work 40 hours a week." [R] (2025)
- "if somebody else is working 50 hours and you're working 100 you'll get twice as much done" [A] **T1** (USC 2014).

## §3. How he'd handle common team situations

Each entry gives his likely move, the evidence, the confidence, when he behaves differently, and how to adapt it to a normal manager's seat. Label any in-voice lines as written in his style.

### 1. An underperformer
- **Move:**
  1. Ask for concrete, recent, personally produced output: what did you get done this week, as bullets or code.
  2. If the answer is vague, process-talk or "we," drill down until it bottoms out.
  3. Give one deadline.
  4. If the bar isn't met, remove or reassign. No slow coaching plan.
  5. If a whole unit lags, replace its leader with a trusted insider.
- **Evidence:** the Agrawal text (2022); the contractor email "now time is up" (2018); the five-bullets email (2025); Raptor (2021); Supercharger (2024); xAI (2026).
- **Confidence:** high for "show me output, then a deadline"; medium for the drill-down script.
- **Differs when:**
  - The person flags a hard physics problem early, with data. Then he takes the problem on himself.
  - The miss is his own schedule.
- **In the user's seat:** ask for the week's output and one hard problem explained to the bottom; set one dated bar. Employment law and documentation apply; instant firing doesn't transfer.

### 2. A missed deadline
- **Move:** he sorts the cause first.
  - **Physics or bad luck, team all-in:** calm, a new aggressive date, often more output (2008).
  - **Slack or a hidden requirement:** a surge. He goes to the bottleneck, holds nightly reviews, sets a weekend target, asks "what's the limiting factor and who owns it?", and deletes the blocker.
- **Evidence:** Raptor 2021; the 2017 "draconian" email; the 2018 tent; Colossus 2024; "least lucky… most foolish part" (T1).
- **Confidence:** high.
- **Note:** he misses his own public dates routinely and defends them ("late, but I always deliver in the end," T1). He judges others by visible urgency, not the date alone.
- **In the user's seat:** name the one limiting factor, put the decision-maker physically at it for a short burst, and reset a tight but credible date stating what was deleted.

### 3. A bloated team
- **Move:**
  1. Freeze hiring.
  2. Require a per-head justification (excellent, necessary, trustworthy) from a named owner by a deadline.
  3. Cut coordinators and overhead before builders.
  4. Offer a clean severance exit.
  5. Expect to rehire ~10% quietly.
  6. Send the survivors a high-bar memo.
- **Evidence:** Tesla 2018 and 2022 (builders excluded); Twitter 2022 (his "~3X the head count per unit of revenue" diagnosis); Tesla 2024; xAI 2025.
- **Confidence:** high.
- **Differs when:** the team is the bottleneck of a product he's scaling. Then he hires (Jan 2026).
- **In the user's seat:** run the three-word test per role with the manager named as owner. Cut coordination layers first. Say up front that a few roles may come back.

### 4. A meeting going nowhere
- **Move:** walk out, or tell non-contributors to leave. If he stays, he forces a decision, an owner and a date. He cancels large or recurring meetings and bans acronyms.
- **Evidence:**
  - "Walk out of a meeting or drop off a call as soon as it is obvious you aren't adding value." (T1)
  - "It is not rude to leave, it is rude to make someone stay and waste their time." (T1)
  - "Excessive meetings are the blight of big companies…" (T1) (all Tesla 2018)
  - The acronym test (T1, 2010)
  - "one person with an accelerator and nine with a set of brakes" [A] (WSJ 2023)
- **Confidence:** high.
- **In the user's seat:** end it, shrink the invite list, send a one-line decision with an owner and a date.

### 5. A requirement nobody can explain
- **Move:** ask who specifically set it (a person, not a department). If nobody can name the person and the reason, delete it now and warn the team that some deletions will come back. A Dilbert-worthy rule gets changed.
- **Evidence:** Lex #438 algorithm lines (T1); the 2018 Dilbert rule and default-deny email; DOGE's "above nothing" field.
- **Confidence:** high.
- **Limit:** he claims to comply with almost all regulation (DealBook 2023). In the user's seat, laws, permits, safety and contracts are **not deletable**. Go after internal policy only.

### 6. A request for more headcount
- **Move:** default no. The requester must show the work can't be deleted, simplified or automated, and compare headcount per unit of output with peers. The exception is a demonstrably exceptional builder at the bottleneck: yes, fast, at whatever price ("Let's go higher").
- **Evidence:** "pause all hiring worldwide" (2022); the 2024 headcount email; the "~3X" text (2022); OpenAI recruiting emails (2015–16); xAI as talent capture (2024).
- **Confidence:** high for both the no and the exception.
- **In the user's seat:** ask for the output-per-head comparison and what gets deleted first. Approve one strong builder over several generalists.

### 7. A star employee who wants to leave
- **Move:** there are no primary records of a retention conversation; this is inferred from his recruiting behavior.
  - **Personal involvement:** he offered to "call them personally" as an "absolute top priority 24/7" against DeepMind counteroffers (2015).
  - **Pay:** "Let's go higher."
  - **The pitch:** mission and the hardest problem. Asked why SpaceX executives stay: "Make Star Trek real."
  - **If they leave anyway:** he moves on, frames it as a stage change ("early-stage team… different from… growth stage," xAI 2026), and backfills from inside his companies.
- **Confidence:** medium.
- **Differs when:**
  - The star joins a rival he's fighting. Expect sharper framing, though no primary example aimed at an individual employee was found.
  - He rarely restructures around one person outside a tiny inner circle (Shotwell).
- **In the user's seat:** a direct conversation from the top within a day; a bigger problem and more upside; no begging. If they still go, part well and promote from inside.

### 8. An employee who contradicts him publicly
- **Move:** high risk of fast removal when the contradiction is public, on his platform, or questions his competence or motives. Private, technical, data-backed disagreement is tolerated and can change his mind.
- **Evidence:** Frohnhoefer and the Slack critics (2022) [unverified]; Achiam (2018) [contested]; the Agrawal cliff (2022) [verified]; the blockchain reversal (2022) [verified]; "Please be super vocal if there is something dumb I'm doing" (T1).
- **Confidence:** medium-high.
- **In the user's seat:** the transferable part is "take it private, bring data." Firing over protected speech or concerted activity carries legal risk; flag it.
