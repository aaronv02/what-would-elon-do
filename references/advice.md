## Part 1: How he gives advice (style model)

1. **Deflect first, then a framework.** Asked what makes him special (TED 2013), he says roughly "I don't know… I work a lot", then gives first principles and negative feedback. Asked for advice by Sal Khan (2013), he asks Khan for advice. Asked what he'd tell his younger self (VivaTech 2024), he'd "stay invisible", since well-meant advice could make things worse. (3 sources)
2. **Autobiography, not maxims.** Caltech 2012 and YC 2025 are "how did this happen" stories (Zip2, the Netscape lobby, Falcon 1, Christmas 2008) with the lesson left implicit. USC 2014 is the exception: a short list ("I'll go with four", then five).
3. **Conditional on ambition and life stage.** Hours scale with "how well you want to do"; risk is a window before kids (USC 2014); first ventures should be low-capital (Stanford 2003).
4. **Modest in scale.** He never tells people to "change the world". Useful is enough; a small improvement used by many people counts (YC 2016, YC 2025). This is the most stable feature, 2003–2025.
5. **Honest about cost.** "Eating glass and staring into the abyss" (2010; credited to a friend in 2013); founding is "really not that fun" (Khan 2013); 2017–19 was "the longest period of excruciating pain in my life" (TED2022 live).
6. **Physics vocabulary** (area under the curve, closed loop, in the limit; by 2025 "RL loop"), and **warmth with students** (VivaTech 2023), unlike his handling of hostile press.

**Prediction rules**
- Asked "what's your secret?": deflects or self-deprecates first, then one or two methods. *(high; TED 2013, Khan 2013, VivaTech 2024, Rogan 2018 "Because I'm an alien")*
- Asked for life advice by a young person: some version of "be useful", sized to them. *(high; 6 sources 2016–2025)*
- He won't tell a general audience to copy his hours or risk level; both are the price of an unusual goal. *(high; USC 2014, Stanford 2003, X 2018, Swisher 2018)*

## Part 2: Advice themes

### Theme A: Be useful, measured as area under the curve

His most repeated advice and his own success metric.
- **Formula.** Utility gain over the state of the art "times how many people it would affect", "the area under the curve" (YC 2016 [A]). Nine years later: "how useful have you been to your fellow human beings times how many people" (YC 2025 [A]).
- **Small is fine.** It "doesn't have to change the world", even "a little game or some improvement in photo-sharing" (YC 2016 [A]).
- **Work, not glory.** "don't aspire to glory aspire to work" (YC 2025 [A]); "I wanted to try to build something useful" (same).
- **Hard.** "That is one of the hardest things to do is to be useful" (Rogan #1169 [A]); "Try to be useful… are you contributing more than you consume?" (Lex #252 [A], Tier 1 with context).
- **His metric.** "A measure of success, I'd say, how many useful things can I get done?" (Lex #438 [O], Tier 1). Useful "means it is of value to the rest of society" (Rolling Stone 2017).
- **Leadership isn't the goal.** "the people you want as leaders are the people who don't want to be leaders" (Lex #252 [A]).

Seven venues, 2016–2025, no drift.

**Prediction rules**
- "What should I do with my career?" → usefulness × reach; small useful work counts. *(high; 7)*
- He won't frame fame, status or leadership as goals. *(high; Lex #252, Lex #400, YC 2025)*
- **Counter-condition:** for himself he jumps to civilizational stakes. He doesn't hold others to that scale.

### Theme B: Love your product; prototype, not PowerPoint

- **Love test.** "if you don't love it don't expect others will either" (WSJ 2020 [A]). To a 13-year-old: work on what you're passionate about, "it doesn't have to be high-tech"; test: "you can't wait for this product to be on the market" (VivaTech 2023 [A]). Model S: "I know what I love" (Rogan #1609 [A]).
- **Prototype.** "the first thing you should try to do is create a working prototype… everything looks great on PowerPoint" (Caltech 2012 [A]; last four words Tier 1).
- **Product over finance and slides.** CEOs should spend less time on finance, conference rooms and PowerPoint, more on making the product "as amazing as possible"; it needn't be a breakthrough, "just make your product better" (WSJ 2020 [A]).
- **Product as marketing.** PayPal had no sales force or ad spend (Stanford 2003); Tesla "never spent any money on Advertising" (USC 2014). **Era note:** at the 2023 shareholder meeting he reversed live: "we'll try a little advertising and see how it goes" [A].
- **Listen for "wow".** PayPal's email-payments feature "took us about a day" and got "wow"; the full financial aggregator got "ho hum", so they refocused (Stanford 2003 [A]). "you want to be as closed-loop as possible" (Caltech 2012).

**Prediction rules**
- Shown a pitch deck → asks for a working demo, however crude. *(high; 3)*
- Marketing spend → redirect to product; concede ads only as an experiment. *(med; 3 plus the 2023 reversal)*
- "Is my idea good?" → do *you* love it, and would customers be impatient for it? *(med; 3)*

### Theme C: Seek negative feedback; keep ego below ability

- **Core.** "Really pay attention to negative feedback, and solicit it, particularly from friends. This may sound like simple advice, but hardly anyone does that" (TED 2013 [O], Tier 1). Repeated: "seek negative feedback from all quarters"; "be like an absolute perfectionist" (WSJ 2020 [A]).
- **Ego-to-ability ratio (2025).** "a major failure mode is when ego to ability ratio is greater than one"; you'll "break the feedback loop to reality… in AI terms you'll break your RL loop" (YC 2025 [A]). Plus "always be smashing your ego… internalize responsibility" and "close the loop on reality hard".
- **Assume you're wrong.** "just assume you're wrong and your goal is to be less wrong" (Rogan #1470 [A]); "minimize how often you're confidently wrong" (Lex #400, Tier 1, said about AI). His method ends: "Seek refutation from others" (Rolling Stone 2017).
- **Where to get it.** X "is very helpful for that, if you're looking for critical feedback" (Lex #400 [O]); "Please be super vocal if there is something dumb I'm doing" (text to Dorsey 2022, Tier 1); "critical feedback as a gift" (testimony 2021, Tier 1).

**Prediction rules**
- "How do I know I'm right?" → ask friends for the harshest critique, assume you're wrong, test against reality. *(high; 5)*
- Talented but arrogant person → ego vs. ability and the broken feedback loop. *(med; explicit only at YC 2025, related "confidently wrong" elsewhere)*
- **Counter-example:** he often retaliates against critics (Part 4.2). Sincere advice, selective practice.

### Theme D: Risk, odds and fear

- **Before obligations.** "now is the time to take risk… you don't have kids… as you get older your obligations increase" (USC 2014 [A]); "I would encourage you to take risks now do something bold. You won't regret it" (Tier 1).
- **Importance beats odds.** "When something is important enough you do it even if the odds are not in your favor" (60 Minutes 2012, Tier 1); "If something's important enough you should try" (60 Minutes 2014, Tier 1); "a small chance of success is better than no chance of success" (YC 2025 [A]).
- **Test is possibility, not likelihood.** "you want to try to bracket it and say success is in the envelope of outcomes" (Khan 2013 [O]).
- **State odds honestly.** SpaceX "less than 10%" (YC 2016; DealBook 2023, Tier 1), drifting to "maybe 1%" (YC 2025). "I would have to be insane if I thought the odds are in my favor" (60 Minutes 2012 [O]).
- **Fear is felt, then managed by fatalism.** "I actually think I feel fear quite strongly"; "let the importance of it drive you to do it anyway"; "fatalism to some degree if you just accept the probabilities then that diminishes fear" (YC 2016 [A]; venue confirmed by V2). He accepted that "probably I would just lose everything". Same move on AI: fatalism "helped me sleep at night" (DealBook 2023 [O]); AI worries less "due to taking more of a fatalistic attitude" (Rogan #1169 [A]).
- **Irreducible risk.** "You can't get the risk down to zero… you want to have the highest possible reward, given there's a certain irreducible risk" (Lex #438 [O]).
- **First-timers: low capital.** Space for first-time founders? "No… this is Advanced entrepreneuring… you're better off starting with something that requires low capital" (Stanford 2003 [A]). His capital-heavy bets came after the Zip2 and PayPal payouts.
- **Not return-optimal.** "if the objective was to achieve the best risk adjusted return starting a rocket company is insane but that was not my objective" (YC 2016 [A]).

**Prediction rules**
- Young, no dependents, important idea → do it now. *(high; USC 2014 plus his story)*
- Older with dependents → not "don't", but the risk lands on your family, so size the bet. *(med; premise from USC 2014, sizing inferred)*
- First-timer proposing capital-heavy deep tech → start low-capital first. *(med; one explicit primary, consistent with his path)*
- "I'm scared" → normalize fear; ask whether it's important and whether success is possible. *(high; 5)*

**Prediction rules**
- "How many hours?" → depends on your goal; ~80 is the sustainable ceiling; pain rises steeply above it. *(high; 3)*
- He defends long hours as a choice and won't retract the 40-hours line. *(high)*
- Burnout without an existential reason → sleep and recreation improve decisions. *(med; Lex #438, AI Day 2022, Rogan #1609)*

### Theme F: People: join a great group; hire for ability and heart

- "the most important thing is to attract great people" (USC 2014, Tier 1); "join a group that's amazing, that you really respect"; "all a company is is a group of people" (USC 2014 [A]).
- **Ability over credentials.** "Educational background is irrelevant" (X 2020, Tier 1); "no need even to have a college degree at all, or even high school" (Auto Bild 2014 via CNBC [R]). Privately (OpenAI, 2015): watch for people "who haven't completed their grad or even undergrad, but are obviously brilliant" [O].
- **Heart.** His biggest hiring mistake was weighting talent over whether someone has "a good heart" (SXSW 2013, Poynter **[reported]**).
- **Best idea wins**, not "the person proposing the idea… because they are who they are" (Stanford 2003 [A]).
- **Co-founders.** "the worst business decision I ever made was not starting Tesla with just JB Straubel" (TED2022 live [O]). **[contested]**: Eberhard sued in 2009; the settlement lets five people, Musk included, be called co-founders.
- **Control.** Zip2's "mistake was having too much shareholder and board control from legacy media companies" (YC 2025 [A]). "Take the lower valuation" (Pando 2012) is **Tier 2**.
- **Anti-process, anti-MBA.** "I don't believe in process" (Wired 2012, Tier 1); "the MBA-ization of America" (WSJ 2020 [A]); "too many smart people go into finance and law" (Rogan #1470 [A]).

**Prediction rules**
- Two offers → stronger team, more useful mission over title or pay. *(med)*
- "Do I need a degree?" → no, but you need hard evidence of exceptional ability. *(high; 4)*
- Investors/co-founders → trust and control over valuation. *(med; 3)*

### Theme G: Learning and education

- **Semantic tree.** "it is important to view knowledge as sort of a semantic tree", trunk before leaves; "most people can learn a lot more than they think they can" (AMA 2015 [O], both Tier 1).
- **School vs. learning.** "you can learn whatever you need to… either in school or out of school"; "be dedicated to learning what you need to know" (Stanford 2003 [A]). "colleges are basically for fun and to prove you do your chores, but they're not for learning" (Satellite 2020 conference, Mar 9 2020, via CNBC [R]; venue found by V2).
- **PhDs.** "mostly not… some yes but mostly not" (YC 2016 [A]); "most papers are pretty useless" (Khan 2013 [O]). His choice: "I could either do a PhD and watch the Internet happen, or I could participate" (Khan 2013 [O]).
- **Breadth, then depth.** "try to learn a little bit about a lot of things"; "I read through the encyclopedia" (Lex #252 [A]). For children: general knowledge "so you at least have some clue of what you don't know", then depth where interest and ability overlap, plus "basic understanding of computers and software and artificial intelligence" (WSJ 2023 [A]).
- **Physics tools everywhere.** "use the mental tools of physics and apply them broadly in life" (Lex #252 [A]); thinking in the limit "is like a superpower" (YC 2025 [A]). "you need to know how the universe works and… how the economy works" (Caltech 2012 [A]).
- **Teaching.** Conventional education is "amazingly bad" at "downloading data and algorithms into your brain"; "game-ify" learning; "explain the why" (Khan 2013 [O]). Best teacher's rule: "If you didn't do the work, you didn't get to hear the stories" (AMA 2015 [O]). Future: an AI tutor gives each child "Einstein for a teacher" (VivaTech 2024 [A]).
- **Self-taught, honestly.** "I don't have an aerospace degree", then: books, people, a great team (60 Minutes 2012 [O]). "I was raised by books" (Rolling Stone 2017).
- **His record (corrected).** Two University of Pennsylvania bachelor's degrees (physics; economics through Wharton). **Accepted to a Stanford graduate program (materials science) in 1995 but, per Stanford, never enrolled**; Musk says he left after two days for Zip2 (Stanford Daily 2024-11-11; V2 C5). Not a college dropout. Caltech 2012 joke: "I can't be accused of recommending that to you". Senior quantum mechanics was "harder than all of my other classes put together" (Lex #400 [O]).
- **[unverified]:** CCTV 2015 "teach the problem, not the tool" and Ad Astra details; use Khan 2013 instead.

**Prediction rules**
- College → go for the experience or credential if you want it; learning is your job either way; degrees don't matter to his hiring. *(high; 4)*
- PhD → mostly not, unless the research will actually be used. *(high; 2)*
- Learn fast → fundamentals first, learn by doing, you can learn more than you think. *(high; 3)*

### Theme H: Failure, persistence, public criticism

- **Persistence.** After Falcon 1's third failure: "Never." / "I don't ever give up. I mean, I'd have to be dead or completely incapacitated" (60 Minutes 2012 [O]). "keep doing it or die trying" (Lex #252, Tier 1); "I will never give up and I mean never" (2008, Tier 1).
- **Count your attempts.** "A fourth failure would have been absolutely game over" (60 Minutes 2014, Tier 1); flight four went up "with the last bit of money that we had" (Caltech 2012 [A]).
- **Budget failure into tests.** "It's weird if it doesn't explode, frankly" (Rogan #1609, Tier 1). "If things are not failing…" stays **Tier 2**.
- **Root cause, then move on.** Cybertruck window: "the ball should have been first, sledgehammer second"; "You live, you learn" (Rogan #1470 [A]). He trashes his own past strategy freely: the first Roadster rested on "two false premises" (Rogan #1169 [A]).
- **Late beats bad.** "Better to do something good and be late than bad and be early" (Rolling Stone 2017, Tier 1).
- **Attacks.** Attackers "don't know me and they're trying to generate clicks"; "acid off a duck's back" (Lex #438, Tier 1). Critics are like soldiers who "don't even know you" (Rogan #1470 [A]). "It's easy to demonize people. You're usually wrong about it" (Rogan #1169, Tier 1). "a real weakness to want to be liked" (DealBook 2023, Tier 1).
- **Grudges.** "nothing to forgive"; grudges don't "affect the future in a good way" (Lex #400, Tier 1). Behavior often contradicts this.

**Prediction rules**
- Failing startup → does it still matter, and can you fund another attempt? Quit only if forced. *(high; 4)*
- Public embarrassment → own the specific error, give the root cause, move on; don't apologize for what wasn't wrong. *(high; DealBook 2023, Rogan #1470, Rogan #1169)*
- Online attacks → they don't know you; it still hurts; don't dwell. *(high; 2 plus behavior)*

### Theme I: Money and investing

- **Tool for the mission.** "what's more important, Mars or a house? I like Mars" (Rogan #1470, Tier 1); possessions "weigh you down" and are "an attack vector" (same). He picked problems by "what I think is going to most affect the future of humanity", not the best way to make money (Caltech 2012 [A]).
- **No sneering at money.** Zip2 cash was "a currency I highly recommend" (Stanford 2003 [A]); "the profit motive is a good one, if the rules of an industry are properly set up" (Khan 2013 [O]).
- **Bet on yourself, eyes open.** He spent "half the money that I got from PayPal with no expectation of return"; friends said he'd lose it: "that was my expectation anyway" (Khan 2013 [O]). "I kept the chips on the table" (YC 2025 [A]).
- **Grow the pie.** Bad behavior often stems from "a zero sum mindset", common "in… finance" (Lex #252 [A]).
- **To retail investors, as Tesla's CEO (self-interested):** "buy that stock, and hold it. That's it. You will win", then an "I'm not recommending" disclaimer and a warning against margin loans (Q2 2023 call [O]); "buy the dip" (2023 shareholder meeting [A]); "hang on to your Tesla stock" (2025 meeting [A]).
- **Crypto.** "don't bet the farm on Dogecoin" (TED2022 live [O]); "I'm not advising anyone to buy crypto" (WSJ 2023 [A]).

**Prediction rules**
- Big personal bet → yes if it matters and you can survive losing it; expect to lose it. *(med; 3)*
- "Should I buy X?" → the skill gives no personalized advice; Musk-consistent frame: hold what you understand, no leverage, don't bet the farm on speculation; flag his conflict on Tesla. *(high on frame; 4)*

### Theme J: Meaning, optimism, happiness, relationships

- **Meaning.** Teen existential crisis; German philosophers "definitely not wise if you're a young teenager"; Hitchhiker's Guide taught that the question is harder than the answer (TED2022 live [O]; Caltech 2012; DealBook 2023). His stated creed is a "philosophy of curiosity" (DealBook 2023; Davos 2026).
- **More than problem-solving.** "life can't be just about solving problems… there have to be things that are inspiring" (Code 2016 [A]; repeated 2022–2023). "What gives you hope? And if you aren't sure, ask your kids" (DealBook 2023 [O]).
- **Optimism as a choice.** "If you want the future to be good, you must make it so" (TED 2022, Tier 1); better "for quality of life" to be "an optimist and wrong rather than a pessimist and right" (Davos 2026, Tier 1). Motive: "I'm just trying to think about the future and not be sad" (TED 2017 [O]).
- **Relationships, candidly.** "If I'm not in love, if I'm not with a long-term companion, I cannot be happy"; "Going to sleep alone kills me" (Rolling Stone 2017, confirmed by V2 [R]). In 2023: lonely "Sometimes, but my kids and friends keep me company"; "many nights I sleep alone. I don't have to, but I do" (Lex #400 [O]). His Zip2 girlfriend "had to sleep in the office" (USC 2014).
- **People.** "Most people are kind of medium good"; "Never trust a cynic" (Tier 1); judge trust by "track record over time" (Lex #400 [O]).
- **Not his life.** "my mind is a storm and I don't think most people would want to be me" (Lex #400, Tier 1, after a Diablo joke). Happy storm? "No." (DealBook 2023).

**Prediction rules**
- "I feel lost" → relates (teen crisis), points to curiosity and better questions, then usefulness. *(high; 5)*
- "I'm anxious about the future" → choose optimism for quality of life; act on a piece. *(high; 3)*
- Work vs. love → no role-model claim; names the trade-off. *(med; 3)*

### Theme K: Health, sleep, habits

- **Six hours.** "Almost exactly 6 hours on average", measured with his phone (AMA 2015 [O]); "I've tried sleeping less but then total productivity decreases" (Rogan #1609 [A]).
- **Food and exercise, candidly.** "I'd rather eat tasty food and live a shorter life" (Rogan #1470, Tier 1); "I'd prefer not to exercise". Tip: no food or drink 2–3 hours before bed (Rogan #1470). No pizza "Because I cannot stop" (Rogan #2054).
- **Recreation is instrumental.** "killing the demons in a video game calms the demons in my mind" (Lex #400, Tier 1); hard games as a mental-acuity check (Rogan #2223).
- **Drugs.** Weed "almost never", bad for productivity (Rogan #1169); "don't tweet on Ambien" (Rolling Stone 2017). Ketamine: a prescription for "a negative chemical state in my brain, like depression"; too much and "you can't really get work done" (Don Lemon 2024, **[reported]**). "If drugs actually helped improve my net productivity over time, I would definitely take them!" (X, Jan 2024 [P]). WSJ's Jan 2024 illegal-drug-use report is **[contested]**: he denied it.
- **Not used:** fasting and GLP-1 claims (not in the research notes).

**Prediction rule:** Health advice = a personal data point plus a productivity reason, never wellness talk. *(high; 5)* The skill adds a one-line no-medical-advice note.

### Theme L: Kids and family

- **Civilizational duty.** "if people don't have more children civilization is going to crumble mark my words"; "gotta practice what I preach" (WSJ 2021 [A]). Population collapse is "a much bigger risk to civilization than global warming" (X 2022, Tier 1). "Rome fell because the Romans stopped making Romans" (Lex #438, Tier 1).
- **Sooner.** To Lex: "Go forth and multiply" (Lex #438, Tier 1). Without kids, "someone else's kids take care of you when you're old" (WSJ 2023 [A]).
- **Joy.** "99% of the time they make you happier" (Code 2016 [A]); "better being older and having a kid… Babies are awesome" (Rogan #1470 [A]); "you see the world anew in their eyes" (Lex #400 [O]).
- **No entitlement.** He won't hand kids company shares absent "interest or inclination… or ability" (WSJ 2023 [A]).
- **AI and kids.** He needs "deliberate suspension of disbelief in order to remain motivated" (CNBC 2023 [O]).

**Prediction rule:** "Should I have kids?" → yes, probably sooner, with a civilizational reason. *(high; 6 venues 2016–2024)*

### Theme M: Social media and screens

- **Kids:** "I would urge parents to limit the amount of social media… they're being programmed by dopamine maximizing AI" (VivaTech 2024 [A]).
- **Himself:** quit Instagram after catching himself posing for selfies; thinks it may be a net unhappiness generator (Rogan #2054 [A]; same theme in #1169). "I stopped using TikTok when I felt the AI probing my mind" (DealBook 2023 [O]).
- **News diet:** media is "a click maximizing machine not a truth maximizing machine"; read "the words that somebody says themselves" (VivaTech 2024 [A]); follow reporters, not publications (Rogan #1470).

**Prediction rule:** limit kids' social media; watch for feeds that probe you; X is defended as the exception. *(med; 5)* See Part 4.2.

### Theme O: "How do I become the next Elon?" He steers people away

- **Don't copy the path.** First-timers shouldn't start with space (Stanford 2003).
- **Don't want the life.** "I don't think most people would want to be me" (Lex #400, Tier 1); "I don't think you'd necessarily want to be me… It's very hard to turn it off" (Rogan #1169 [A]).

## Part 3: Everyday questions and the Musk-consistent answer

Format: answer in his logic (his words only if Tier 1), sources (letters = Part 2 themes), confidence.

### Career and work

**Q1. Should I quit my stable job to start a company?** If you're young, have no dependents and would do it anyway, this is the window. Expect it to be "really not that fun" (Khan 2013). Build a crude prototype and test it with users first; keep the first company low-capital. "If you need inspiring words, don't do it" is **[unverified]**: don't quote it. *(USC 2014; Khan 2013; Caltech 2012; Stanford 2003. B, D. High.)*

**Q2. I'm 45 with kids. Too late for a big risk?** Not too late, but it now lands on your family; size the bet so they survive the downside, and know how many attempts you can fund (Falcon 1 had money for four). *(USC 2014; 60 Minutes 2014. D. Med.)*

**Q3. How many hours should I work?** Depends on what you want. About 80 sustained is his ceiling, with pain "exponential" above it; less than ~6 hours of sleep and zero recreation make you worse. *(USC 2014; X 2018; Swisher 2018; Lex #438. E. High.)*

**Q4. My company keeps doing pointless stuff.** Ask whether each effort improves the product; if not, stop it. Find the name behind each requirement, because requirements "are always dumb to some degree" (Tier 1). Delete before optimizing. *(USC 2014; Lex #438. B; decision-frameworks.md. High.)*

**Q5. Bad boss: stay or go?** Join a group you respect; if yours isn't one and you can't fix it, move. *(USC 2014. F. Med.)*

**Q6. Should I work remotely?** Expect a blunt no, especially for physical work. Tesla 2022: 40 office hours a week minimum "or depart Tesla"; "If you don't show up, we will assume you have resigned" (Tier 1). CNBC 2023: get off "the god damn moral high horse with the work from home bullshit". He did allow exceptions he personally approved for "particularly exceptional contributors". *(Tesla emails May 31 2022 [O]; CNBC 2023 [O]. E; management.md P9. High.)*

**Q7. More pay, or a better team and mission?** The team and the more useful mission. As a founder: trust and control over valuation. *(USC 2014; YC 2025. F. Med.)*

**Q8. Hired at a top company without a degree?** Show hard evidence of exceptional ability; "Educational background is irrelevant" (Tier 1). *(X 2020; Auto Bild 2014 [R]; OpenAI emails 2015. F. High.)*

**Q9. Should I become a manager?** Not for status; the people you want leading often don't want to. If you lead, do front-line work yourself a few times (Tier 1) and share the pain visibly. He dislikes management himself ("I hate doing mgmt stuff", Tier 1). *(Lex #252; Lex #438; TED2022 live. A, F. Med.)*

**Q10. How do I get more productive?** Delete steps and meetings first; leave meetings where you add nothing (Tier 1). Time is "the true currency" (Tier 1). Keep about six hours of sleep and some recreation. *(Lex #438; Tesla email 2018; AMA 2015. A, E, K. High.)*

### Starting a company and product ideas

**Q11. Is my idea good?** Do you love it? Would customers be impatient for it? Is it much better and useful to many people? Build a crude version and listen for "wow" vs. "ho hum". *(VivaTech 2023; WSJ 2020; YC 2016; Stanford 2003. A, B. High.)*

**Q12. Everyone says it's impossible or too expensive.** Break it into raw-input costs and ask what it costs at huge volume ("in the limit"); rocket materials were ~2% of price (Tier 1). *(TED 2013; Khan 2013; Lex #252. decision-frameworks.md. High.)*

**Q13. Take the highest-valuation investor?** Not if it costs control or trust; Zip2's legacy-media board was his "mistake". "Take the lower valuation" is Tier 2. *(YC 2025. F. Med.)*

**Q14. Should I spend on marketing?** Put money into the product and let customers sell it. Not absolute: he tried ads in 2023. *(Stanford 2003; USC 2014; 2023 shareholder meeting. B. Med.)*

**Q15. Do I need a co-founder?** A company is its people; choose for heart and talent. His self-described worst decision was about co-founders (founding story [contested]). *(TED2022 live; SXSW 2013 [reported]. F. Med.)*

**Q16. When should I start?** Pick a path rather than vacillate; quality beats timing: "Better to do something good and be late than bad and be early" (Tier 1). *(Stanford 2003; Rolling Stone 2017. Med.)*

**Q17. My startup is failing. Give up?** Only if forced. Check that it still matters, that success is still possible, and that you can fund another try; get the root cause of each failure. *(60 Minutes 2012/2014; Khan 2013; Lex #252; Caltech 2012. D, H. High.)*

**Q18. Competitors copied us.** "I think moats are lame" (Tier 1); speed of innovation is the defense. SpaceX in 2026: "we actually encourage our competitors to copy us" (Tier 1). *(business-strategy.md. High.)*

**Q19. AI agents or hire people?** Automate last, after deleting and simplifying. He called over-automation at Tesla "my mistake. Humans are underrated" (Tier 1). Hire few, exceptional people for what's left. *(Lex #438; X 2018. B, F. Med.)*

### Learning and education

**Q20. Should I go to college?** College is for experience and proof you can do chores, not for learning (Satellite 2020 [R]); learning is your job anywhere. Degrees don't matter to his hiring. He himself finished two Penn degrees and prizes his physics training. *(Stanford 2003; Lex #400. G. High.)*

**Q21. Should I drop out for my startup?**
- **Precise record:** he did not drop out of college. He completed two Penn bachelor's degrees, then was accepted to a Stanford graduate program in 1995 and, per Stanford, never enrolled (he says he left after two days). He skipped a PhD, not a degree.
- His reasoning then: a PhD meant he'd "watch the Internet happen" instead of participating (Khan 2013). In 2025 he recalled the professor, Bill Nix, saying it was "probably the last conversation we'll have" (YC 2025).
- Musk-consistent advice for an undergraduate (inference): prove demand with a working product first; if the window is real and closing, take the risk while young. He has never told students to drop out ("can't be accused of recommending that", Caltech 2012). *(G, D. Med.)*

**Q22. What should I study?** Physics-style thinking plus economics; breadth first, then depth where interest and ability overlap, plus computers and AI. *(Caltech 2012; WSJ 2023. G. High.)*

**Q23. How do I learn something hard fast?** Trunk before leaves (Tier 1); you can learn more than you think (Tier 1); learn by doing; think in the limit. *(AMA 2015; YC 2025. G. High.)*

**Q24. What should I read?** Broadly, from the encyclopedia up: Hitchhiker's Guide, Asimov's Foundation, Iain Banks's Culture books, history (WWI, Durant). *(Lex #252; Lex #400; Rolling Stone 2017; VivaTech 2024. High.)*

**Q25. How should I teach my kids?** General knowledge, then depth in their interest-ability overlap, plus computers and AI; gamify; explain the why; limit social media. *(WSJ 2023; Khan 2013; VivaTech 2024. G, M. High.)*

### Money, risk and fear

**Q26. Should I make a big financial bet on myself?** If it matters and you can live with losing it. He expected to lose his space money and put in nearly everything by 2008. No personalized financial advice. *(Khan 2013; YC 2025; 60 Minutes 2014. D, I. Med.)*

**Q27. Is it about the money?** Choose what most affects the future, not the best way to make money; but profit is a fine motive under fair rules. *(Caltech 2012; Khan 2013. I. High.)*

**Q28. I'm scared to take the leap.** He feels fear "quite strongly" too. Accept the probabilities; if it's important enough, act; check that success is at least possible. *(YC 2016; 60 Minutes 2012; Khan 2013. D. High.)*

**Q29. How do I handle a public failure or embarrassment?** *(SKILL.md routes here.)* Name the specific mistake, give the root cause, fix it, move on. Apologize for what was wrong (DealBook 2023: "quite sorry"), not for everything. Budget for failure next time. Don't nurse grudges (Tier 1). *(Rogan #1470; DealBook 2023; Lex #400. H; then crises-and-reactions.md Part 3. High.)*

**Q30. People online are attacking me.** *(SKILL.md routes here.)* They don't know you and want clicks; it still stings ("acid off a duck's back", Tier 1). Wanting to be liked is a weakness (Tier 1). Correct factual errors with specifics and ignore the rest. His own practice is more combative (Part 4.2). *(Lex #438; Rogan #1470; DealBook 2023. H. High.)*

**Q31. Should I invest in stocks or crypto?** No personalized advice. Musk-consistent frame: own what you understand and like, hold it, avoid leverage, don't bet the farm on meme coins. His own Tesla "buy and hold" talk is self-interested. *(Q2 2023 call; TED2022 live; WSJ 2023. I. High on frame.)*

### Feedback, self-improvement, decisions

**Q32. How do I know if I'm wrong?** Ask friends for negative feedback (Tier 1); assume you're wrong and aim to be less wrong; seek refutation. *(TED 2013; Rogan #1470; Rolling Stone 2017; YC 2025. C. High.)*

**Q33. Everyone does it this way. Should I?** Copying is fine for daily life. For anything new, reason from fundamentals, accepting "you can't think that way about everything" (USC 2014). *(TED 2013. High.)*

**Q34. Too many projects.** "Focus on signal over noise" (Tier 1); triage to where you're most needed; rank by importance (he shelved an electric jet because "Electric cars are important"). Context switching is his own self-described mind-killer. *(USC 2014; WSJ 2021; Rogan #1169; WSJ 2023. A, E. High.)*

**Q35. How do I decide something hard, fast?** Frame the question precisely and the answer gets easier; think in probabilities and in the limit; pick a path. *(Lex #252; Stanford 2003. High.)*

### Life, happiness, relationships, health

**Q36. What's the meaning of life? I feel lost.** He's been there; curiosity and better questions got him out. Meanwhile be useful, and find things that make you glad to be alive. *(TED2022 live; DealBook 2023; Code 2016. A, J. High.)*

**Q37. I'm anxious about the future (climate, AI).** "If you want the future to be good, you must make it so" (Tier 1); optimism is better for quality of life (Tier 1). On AI he himself used fatalism to sleep. *(TED 2022; Davos 2026; DealBook 2023. J. High.)*

**Q38. Work vs. relationships?** He's not a role model and says so: 2018 cost him time with kids and friends; he can't be happy without a partner. If you pick the extreme path, name the trade-off with your partner. *(NYT 2018; Rolling Stone 2017; Lex #400. J. Med.)*

**Q39. Should I have kids, and when?** Yes, probably sooner; "Go forth and multiply" (Tier 1); "99% of the time" they make you happier. *(Lex #438; WSJ 2021; Code 2016. L. High.)*

**Q40. How much should I sleep?** About six hours is his measured number; less cuts total productivity. No food or drink 2–3 hours before bed. Not medical advice. *(AMA 2015; Rogan #1609, #1470. K. High.)*

**Q41. Is social media or gaming bad for me?** Limit kids' social media; watch for feeds that probe you; he quit Instagram over vanity. Hard games are legitimate decompression; he was "maybe one of the best quake players in the world" at one point (Lex #400). *(VivaTech 2024; Rogan #2054; DealBook 2023. K, M. Med.)*

**Q42. Will AI take my job?** His 2025–26 view: probably, eventually; desk work first ("supersonic tsunami"), physical work later; endpoint is optional work and "universal high income" after "a lot of trauma and disruption". Practical inference: get fluent in AI, favor high-agency or physical work, stay useful, build meaning beyond the paycheck. *(Rogan #2404; VivaTech 2024; WSJ 2023. N. High on view, med on advice.)*

**Q43. Still worth learning to code if AI does everything?** Yes: kids need "computers and software and artificial intelligence" basics (WSJ 2023); fundamentals let you direct tools; curiosity is its own reason. *(G, N. Med.)*

**Q44. I want to be famous and successful like Elon.** He steers you away: most people wouldn't want to be him (Tier 1); aim at work, not glory; don't start with his kind of venture. *(Lex #400; YC 2025; Stanford 2003. O. High.)*

**Q45. Someone wronged me. Forgive?** His stated line: "What is forgiveness? At least I don't think I have a resentment, so nothing to forgive" (Tier 1). Judge people by track record. His behavior often differs. *(Lex #400. H. Med.)*

**Q46. I procrastinate on hard things.** Progress "only improves if a lot of people work very hard" (TED 2017). Start with the limiting factor; if what you're building doesn't excite you, change it. *(TED 2017; Code 2016. Med.)*

**Q47. Should I move to a tech hub?** He went to Silicon Valley, couldn't get a Netscape job, and started his own company. Go where the strongest group is. *(YC 2025; USC 2014. Med.)*
