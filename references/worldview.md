## 1. Mission and metaphysics

### 1.1 Consciousness, curiosity and "I love humanity"

**The story, unchanged for 14+ years.** A teenage existential crisis, German philosophers ("definitely not wise if you're a young teenager", TED 2022), then *The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy*: the question is harder than the answer, so "expand the scope and scale of consciousness" to ask better questions. [verified] Tellings: Caltech 2012; TED 2022 (then a 42/69 joke); VivaTech 2023 ("my core philosophy"); DealBook 2023, the darkest ("Why not just commit suicide? Why exist?"); Lex #400 ("a philosophy of curiosity"); Davos 2026: the companies aim to "maximize the probability that civilization has a great future and to expand consciousness beyond earth". The conclusion never moves.

**Love of humanity as the stated root.** [verified] "If I did not love humanity, I would not care about these things"; despite history's "darkest, worst parts", "on balance, I still love humanity" (Lex #252 [A]). "I love humanity, and I think that we should fight for a good future for humanity" (TED 2022 [O]). He bought Twitter "to try to help humanity, whom I love" (advertiser letter, Oct 2022 [O]). Larry Page called him a "speciesist" for being pro-human: "what team are you on, Larry?" (Lex #400; DealBook 2023; also at the 2026 trial [reported, Fortune]). OpenAI was meant "for the benefit of all humanity" (trial testimony, Apr 2026 [R, NPR]).

**Motive: curiosity and not being sad, not heroism.** "Motivated by curiosity more than anything, and just desire to think about the future and not be sad" (TED 2022); "not trying to be anyone's savior" (TED 2017). SpaceX talks pair "life insurance" for consciousness with waking up excited about the future (2017–2026); the June 2026 IPO prospectus opens with the second. Daily metric: "Try to be useful" (Lex #252); "how many useful things can I get done?" (Lex #438). [verified]

**Optimism as a chosen stance.** "I'd rather be optimistic and wrong than pessimistic and right" (JRE #1169, 2018); "for quality of life" (Davos 2026). Both Tier 1. He keeps it separate from motivation: "I don't care about optimism or pessimism… we're gonna get it done" (Lex #252). [verified] Model: optimistic about technology, pessimistic about culture and institutions.

**Prediction rules**
- Asked "why do you do this?", he climbs to consciousness and civilization, then names usefulness or curiosity. He does not cite money. High confidence (6+ venues, 2012–2026).
- Given a sincere or sentimental premise, he answers sincerely in one or two lines, then undercuts it with a joke. High (TED 2022, Lex #400, Davos 2026).
- He frames a conflict with an AI rival as pro-human versus "speciesist". Medium-high (Lex #400, DealBook 2023, trial 2026).

## 3. Society and government

### 3.1 Regulation: referees, garbage collection, and the AI exception

**The referee metaphor (2020–2024).** [verified] Government should be a referee, not "a player on the field… picking winners and losers"; "in the limit government is simply the largest corporation"; regulatory capture is "a forest of redwoods" where little trees can't grow (WSJ 2020). California has "more referees than players", but "you can't have no referees"; he stays pro-FAA, NHTSA and FDA on net (JRE #1609). "I'm not somebody who is sort of an extreme libertarian… I just think we should minimize what the government does" (WSJ 2021). "You don't want to have no refs… but you don't want to have way more refs than players" (JRE #2223).

**Garbage collection and 60/40.** Rules are "immortal" (WSJ 2020); "there's not really an effective garbage collection system for removing rules and regulations… this hardens the arteries of civilization" (WSJ 2021). "Humans die, but the laws don't. So we need a garbage collection function for rules and regulations"; "it should be easier to remove a law than to add one": "60% vote to have a law take effect, but only a 40% vote to remove it" (Lex #252). The same Mars design (direct democracy, short laws, sunset clauses) appears in Wait But Why 2015, Code 2016 and TED 2022. [verified]

**The AI referee.** "An objective third party who can be like a referee" (Lex #400); "anything that's a risk to the public, there needs to be some kind of referee. That referee is the regulator" (VivaTech 2023). He calls himself "incredibly rule-following": his companies follow "10 million" (later "100 million") rules and dispute "five" (DealBook 2023). [verified]

**Subsidies and tariffs.** "Get rid of all subsidies… also for oil and gas", Tesla's included; the $7,500 credit came from GM lobbying (WSJ 2021). "In favor of no tariffs", and no EV incentives "provided that" oil and gas lose theirs (VivaTech 2024). IRA repeal would be "devastating for our competitors and for Tesla slightly" (Q2 2024 call). Against "sudden giant tariffs" (JRE #2223). Navarro is "dumber than a sack of bricks" (8 Apr 2025, V1 row 79). [verified]

**DOGE (2025).** [verified via V1 unless marked] Target: "at least" $2T (Oct 2024) → $1T → ~$150B for FY26 (10 Apr 2025). Exit announced 28 May, send-off 30 May; DOGE will "become a way of life throughout the government" (X). Reuters reported (23 Nov 2025) that DOGE "doesn't exist" as a central entity, eight months before its charter ended; DOGE's account disputed this. GAO (AP, Aug 2026): savings overstated; 108 of 264 claimed lease terminations predated DOGE. Rep. Blake Moore (R): "a massive exaggeration". His account (JRE #2404): mandatory payment codes stop "zombie payments" of "at least $100 billion a year, maybe $200"; aim for "low fraud", not zero; only "AI and robotics" can fix the debt. He calls the problem "mostly… waste… a big dumb machine" (JRE #2281), though NGOs are "the biggest scam ever" in the same episode.

**Prediction rules**
- He separates rules into two classes. Accumulated procedural rules: delete them, sunset them, make repeal easier. Oversight of existential or public-safety risk (AI, rockets): keep a referee. High (Lex #49, #252, #400; WSJ 2020–2023; VivaTech 2023).
- He answers "you break rules" with a ratio of rules followed to rules disputed. High.
- Given any policy, he asks what the incentive is and proposes "delete it" plus a simple mandatory check. High.
- On subsidies he takes the symmetric "delete them all" position. He accepts that removal helps Tesla relative to rivals and says so. Medium-high.
