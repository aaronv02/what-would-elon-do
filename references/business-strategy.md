## Part B. Case studies

### 1. The "Secret" Tesla Master Plan (Aug 2, 2006) [verified; read in full]
- **Problem.** EV technology had high unit cost and tiny volume, and no startup had built a car company at scale in decades.
- **His move.** Enter at the top with the Roadster and publish the whole plan. The closing list runs: build a sports car, use that money for an affordable car, then a more affordable one, provide zero-emission power, and "Don't tell anyone."
- **His stated reasoning.**
  - "The strategy of Tesla is to enter at the high end of the market… and then drive down market as fast as possible" [O].
  - "When someone buys the Tesla Roadster sports car, they are actually helping pay for development of the low cost family car." [O]
  - He showed the math rather than claiming it: an efficiency chain ending at 52.5%, measured against a gas plant on purpose.
  - He disclosed his conflict on SolarCity, calling himself its "principal financier", and credited "Martin and the rest of the team."
- **Result.** The plan ran Roadster → Model S → Model 3 → Model Y. Model Y was the world's best-selling vehicle of 2023 (~1.23M, per JATO), and Tesla delivered 1,808,581 cars that year [verified]. In 2016 he wrote that the blog had "pretty much completely failed" at its other goal, stopping attack articles.
- **Lesson.** When unit cost must fall with scale, sell first to buyers who pay for novelty, and fund the cost curve with their margin. Publish the long game and show the arithmetic.

### 2. Master Plan Part Deux (July 20, 2016) and SolarCity [verified]
- **Problem.** Part 1 was nearly done. Tesla needed a story covering solar, more segments and autonomy, and wanted to buy SolarCity, which he had financed since 2006.
- **His move.** A four-line plan:
  1. Solar roofs with storage.
  2. Every major vehicle segment.
  3. Self-driving "10X safer."
  4. "Enable your car to make money for you when you aren't using it" [O].

  The factory itself would become "a product."
- **His stated reasoning.**
  - "I thought our chances of success were so low that I didn't want to risk anyone's funds in the beginning but my own." [O]
  - Delaying Autopilot would be "morally reprehensible" [O].
  - He set checkable targets: a 5–10x factory gain, 10x safer driving, and ~6 billion miles for regulatory approval.
  - A car cheaper than Model 3 was "unlikely to be necessary," because shared autonomy would cut cost instead [O].
  - At the SolarCity trial: "$3 billion in cash flows for $2 billion. This is a no brainer." [R, AP 2021]
- **Result.** The robotaxi idea came ten years before paid rides. The cheaper-car line came back in 2024, when a dedicated $25k car was dropped for Cybercab. The Delaware Chancery court ruled for Musk on SolarCity (Apr 2022), while finding him "more involved in the process than a conflicted fiduciary should be" [verified].
- **Lesson.** A master plan binds separate bets into one mission story, and that story justifies related-party deals. The numeric targets he states become the yardsticks he is later held to.

### 3. Master Plans 3 (Mar 2023) and 4 (Sep 2025): the altitude rises [verified]
- **Problem.** In 2023 margins were compressing and investors wanted the next car. By 2025 deliveries were falling for a second year.
- **His move.**
  - **Part 3 (Investor Day):** a whole-economy energy model of 240 TWh storage, 30 TW renewables and ~$10T investment. It had no product and no date. He declined next-car questions and held the one hard announcement, Giga Mexico, until after a break.
  - **Part 4:** "sustainable abundance" through AI and robots. Its principles begin "Growth is infinite."
- **His stated reasoning.**
  - He padded Part 3's headline on purpose: the calculation "is more like uh 6 trillion, but… we made it higher to make it 10 trillion." [A]
  - He called it optimism "based on… actual physics and… real calculations" [A].
  - Part 4 is unsigned corporate text with no numbers, no "I", no jokes and no list. That is unlike the 2006 and 2016 plans he signed.
- **Result.** Giga Mexico was later paused [unverified]. Electrek called Part 4 vague [verified]. On the Q4 2025 call the mission moved again, to "amazing abundance" and "universal high income" [O].
- **Lesson.** When the core numbers weaken, he raises the story's abstraction and lengthens its time horizon. Model his voice on the plans he signed, not on Part 4.

### 4. Direct sales, no ads, and the live reversal [verified]
- **Problem.** Dealers profit from service and from gas cars, and incumbents outspend any startup on ads.
- **His move.** Company stores and online sales, fought for state by state. Near-zero ad spend, with Musk as the marketing department.
- **His stated reasoning.**
  - Dealers have "a fundamental conflict of interest" (Tesla blog, 2012) [O].
  - Fame was instrumental from the start: "Nobody will sell me any parts if they don't know who I am." [R, Esquire 2012, as relayed by his mother]
  - Q4 2019: "more than $20 billion in revenue with zero advertising spend" [O].
- **Result.**
  - Tesla has one national price and can change it overnight, which made the 2023 cuts possible.
  - On May 16, 2023 he reversed live: "we'll try a little advertising and see how it goes" [A; wording varies by outlet]. He then told CNBC, "I only just agreed to it. So I don't have a fully formed strategy." [O]
  - The brand is tied to his persona. In Q1 2025 his CFO cited "hostility towards our brand," while Musk said demand had not fallen.
  - Deliveries fell 13% in Q1 2025 and fell again for full-year 2025 (1,636,129, −8.6%) [verified].
- **Lesson.** He removes intermediaries whose incentives diverge from his, and spends attention instead of ad money. He underweights the brand risk of his own persona, and grants cheap concessions live while holding strategy.

### 5. Open patents, open chargers, "copy us" [verified]
- **Problem.** The EV market was too small for him to win big alone.
- **His move.** Openness at three points:
  - the 2014 patent pledge (Tier 1);
  - Superchargers "not intended to be a walled garden" (Q1 2018);
  - the connector opened as NACS, which other automakers adopted [reported].

  FSD licensing offers (2023–24) were dropped without a deal.
- **His stated reasoning.**
  - "Our true competition is not the small trickle of non-Tesla electric cars being produced, but rather the enormous flood of gasoline cars" [O, 2014].
  - A patent "really just meant that you bought a lottery ticket to a lawsuit."
  - On fearing copycats: "We couldn't have been more wrong."
  - "Patents are for the weak" [R, Jay Leno's Garage, 2022].
  - In 2026: "we actually encourage our competitors to copy us. We do nothing to slow them down." [O]
- **Counter-examples.**
  - SpaceX has "essentially no patents… the Chinese would just use them as a recipe book" [O, Wired 2012].
  - He held back at AI Day because rivals study it "on a frame-by-frame basis" (Q4 2023), and delayed Optimus V3 for the same reason (Q1 2026) [O].
  - In April 2024 he fired the ~500-person Supercharger team, pledged "well over $500M," and rehired about a dozen within weeks [verified].
- **Lesson.** He opens what grows the category when the moat is speed and talent, and stays closed where a rival could leapfrog in a winner-take-most race.

### 6. Pricing for volume, 2020–2026 [verified]
- **Problem.** Factories needed volume, interest rates rose, and Chinese competition grew.
- **His move.** Repeated global price cuts from January 2023.
- **His stated reasoning.**
  - Q2 2020: "we're not trying to be super profitable either" [O].
  - Q1 2023: "it's better to ship a large number of cars at a lower margin and subsequently harvest that margin in the future as we perfect autonomy." [O]
  - Q2 2023: "it does make sense to sacrifice margins in favor of making more vehicles" [O].
  - Rate rises are "equivalent to increase in the price of a car" [O].
  - "even small changes in the price have a big effect on demand, very big." [A, Investor Day 2023]
- **Result.**
  - Record deliveries in 2023, then lower margins and two annual declines [verified].
  - The cheap car was redefined, not built. "A regular $25,000 model is pointless… silly"; Cybercab "is a $25,000 car" (Q3 2024) [O]. The affordable models became stripped "Standard" trims, and "Cybercab is the compact vehicle" (Q1 2026).
  - The margin he deferred depends on his weakest forecasts, the autonomy dates (Part G).
- **Lesson.** He prices to elasticity and installed base, and treats each unit sold as an option on future software.

### 7. SpaceX: first-principles cost, government as anchor [verified]
- **Problem (2002).** Launch was expensive because rockets were thrown away.
- **His move.** Build in-house, pursue reuse, and take NASA contracts.
- **His stated reasoning.**
  - Rocket materials cost about 2% of the price (Tier 1, Wired 2012).
  - Cost-plus contracts create "an incentive for it to make everything as expensive as it can possibly justify" [O].
  - With speed fixed by physics, "you're really left with one key parameter… and that's cost." [O]
- **Result.**
  - Falcon 1 failed three times and reached orbit on the fourth try (Sep 2008). NASA awarded the $1.6B CRS contract on Dec 23, 2008.
  - SpaceX flew 165 Falcon 9 launches in 2025, only 8 of them on new boosters. A booster flew a 35th time on June 8, 2026.
  - Raptor 2 cost about half as much as its predecessor. The <$250k figure was a target, not an achieved cost.
  - The Washington Post counted ≥$38B in government contracts, loans, subsidies and credits across his companies, $6.3B of it in 2024 [all verified].
- **Lesson.** Compute the ratio of input cost to price. Where incentives explain the gap, attack the incentive. Use government as an anchor customer.

### 8. Starlink: the cash engine [verified]
- **Problem.** Mars needs huge capital, and launch alone is a small market.
- **His move.** Use SpaceX's own cheap launches to build a constellation and sell broadband directly.
- **His stated reasoning.**
  - "The main reason I am personally accumulating assets is in order to fund this." [O, New Space 2017]
  - To staff in 2024: Starlink "is helping get Humanity to Mars" [A].
  - The funding logic moved from "Kickstarter" jokes (2016), to cannibalizing Falcon/Dragon (2017, which never happened), to Starlink paying for it.
  - In 2026 he argued the bear case himself: even if revenue per bit fell 10x, Starlink revenue would still rise 10x [O].
- **Result (S-1).**
  - Subscribers: 2.3M (2023) → 4.4M → 8.9M → 10.3M (Mar 2026).
  - 2025 revenue was $11.4B (61% of SpaceX), with ~$4.4B operating income.
  - ARPU fell from ~$99 to ~$66 a month [verified].
  - He treats ownership as leverage. To Poland's foreign minister: "Be quiet, small man. You pay a tiny fraction of the cost." (Mar 2025) [verified]
- **Lesson.** Integrate down into the largest profitable market your capability unlocks, let it fund the mission, and accept falling unit price for volume.

### 9. OpenAI, 2015–2018: funding as leverage, control as precondition [verified; court exhibits]
- **Problem.** He wanted a counterweight to DeepMind, which needed talent and huge compute.
- **His move.** He signaled a big number with himself as backstop, put no cap on recruiting money, and demanded initial control in the 2017 for-profit talks. When refused, he left quickly.
- **His stated reasoning.**
  - "We need to go with a much bigger number than $100M to avoid sounding hopeless" (2015). Announce $1B: "I will cover whatever anyone else doesn't provide." [O]
  - "I would unequivocally have initial control of the company, but this will change quickly." and "I'd have a 25% influence, which is my min comfort level." [O, Sep 2017]
  - When his motives were questioned: "Discussions are over." The earlier deal was "no longer on the table." "I will no longer fund OpenAI until you have made a firm commitment" [O].
  - Leaving: "0%. Not 1%." and "This needs billions per year immediately or forget it." [O, Dec 2018]
- **Other side [contested].** Sutskever and Brockman wrote at the time that "absolute control is extremely important to you." Brockman privately described Musk as "fairly non-negotiable" on 50–60% equity, and testified in 2026 that Musk walked out when refused. Musk's emails frame control as temporary.
- **Result.**
  - He left in 2018. Later he said "I fully admit to being a huge idiot here" (Tier 1, CNBC 2023) and "I am the reason OpenAI exists" [O].
  - He founded xAI (2023), sued (2024), and led a $97.4B unsolicited bid (Feb 10, 2025).
  - On May 18, 2026 an advisory jury found his claims time-barred, and the judge adopted the verdict. He called it "a calendar technicality" and vowed to appeal; no appellate ruling yet [verified].
- **Lesson.** He tries to buy control with capital and credibility. If refused, he exits within minutes, withdraws the old offer, and competes or litigates from outside.

### 10. Twitter, 2022: board seat → buyout → diligence as a brake [verified; text exhibits]
- **Problem (his framing).** He saw the "digital town square" as mismanaged and overstaffed.
- **His move.** Five days after accepting a board seat, he answered the CEO's pushback within about a minute (Apr 9) [O]:
  1. "What did you get done this week?"
  2. "This is a waste of time."
  3. "Will make an offer to take Twitter private."

  His private case: purging fake users "will make the numbers look terrible, so restructuring should be done as a private company," and Twitter had "~3X the head count per unit of revenue" [O].
- **Financing.**
  - $54.20 a share (~$44B), with $13B of bank debt and ~19 co-investors putting in ≈$7.1B [verified].
  - He sold ~$23B of Tesla stock in Apr–Dec 2022, largely for the deal. The ~$16.4B he sold in late 2021 was mostly tax-related [verified].
  - To Larry Ellison, who offered $1B: "I'd recommend maybe $2B or more… I'd rather have you than anyone else." [O] Whether Ellison then committed $2B or $1B is [unverified]: the exhibits' reading and the May 2022 commitment list conflict.
- **Diligence as a brake.**
  - Two weeks after signing: "Let's slow down just a few days" and "It won't make sense to buy Twitter if we're headed into WW3" [O].
  - He wrote down the escape condition. Proving 95% of users are real is "an extremely fundamental due diligence item," and the deal "moves forward if it passes due diligence, but obviously not if there are massive gaping issues." [O]
  - Twitter sued, and he closed in late October 2022. In 2023 he said a court would have forced him: "yes that is the reason" [A, BBC].
- **Result.**
  - Staff fell from "just under 8,000" to ~1,500.
  - Advertisers were courted (Oct 2022), told "go fuck yourself" (DealBook 2023), then sued via the GARM case (Aug 2024).
  - Fidelity marked its stake down ~71.5% [all verified]. He has called the deal "way overpriced" [A, Rogan 2024].
  - X folded into xAI at $33B (Mar 2025), then into SpaceX. X Money launched invite-only on Jul 28, 2026, and opened to all US Premium users by Aug 31 [verified].
- **Lesson.** His deference lasts until his standing is challenged; then he changes the game. After committing, he finds a principled-sounding gating condition rather than admit remorse.

### 11. "Funding secured" (2018) [verified]
- **Problem.** Short sellers and quarterly pressure. In 2017 he said being public "actually makes us less efficient" [O].
- **His move.** He tweeted the take-private plan on Aug 7, 2018, then reversed it 17 days later in "Staying Public."
- **His stated reasoning.** The plan "has nothing to do with accumulating control for myself" [O]. The retraction still said there was "more than enough funding" [O].
- **Result.** He settled with the SEC, and later testified the settlement was made "under duress." A 2023 securities jury found for him. On the stand: "It's not a problem for me to raise money… If you do a good job for investors they give you money." [R]
- **Lesson.** He reverses actions but rarely the factual premise. He treats handshake commitments from powerful backers as binding.

### 12. Pay and control at Tesla, 2017–2025 [verified]
- **Problem (his framing).** He would build AI and robots only if he could not be pushed out.
- **His move.** All-or-nothing awards with no salary, plus a demand for ~25% voting control.
- **His stated reasoning.**
  - In 2017 he said the added pay was "just so that I can put as much as possible towards… Mars… This is kinda crazy, but it is true." [O, quoted by the court] He lowered his own ask twice, which the court described as negotiating "against himself."
  - In 2024, on control: "Unless that is the case, I would prefer to build products outside of Tesla." [P] He wants ~25% so that "if I go bonkers… they can throw me out" (Q4 2023).
  - Q3 2025: "It's called compensation, but it's not like I'm going to go spend the money." He called the proxy advisers "corporate terrorists."
- **Result.**
  - **2018 award.** Granted when Tesla was worth ~$59B. Rescinded in Delaware (Jan 2024), re-ratified with a move to Texas (June 2024) [reported]. Restored by the Delaware Supreme Court on Dec 19, 2025, with $1 nominal damages; the award was worth ~$139B. Musk said he was "vindicated."
  - **2025 award.** Passed Nov 6, 2025 with over 75% of shares cast: 423,743,904 shares in 12 tranches. Each tranche pairs a $2T–$8.5T market cap with an operating goal: 20M vehicles, 1M robotaxis, 1M bots, 10M FSD subscriptions, or up to $400B adjusted EBITDA. The last two tranches need a succession plan. It is worth up to ~$1T gross (~$878B net). ISS, Glass Lewis and NBIM opposed it.
  - **Interim award.** 96M shares (Aug 2025) were to be offset once the 2018 award was restored, which it was [verified].
- **Lesson.** The real target is voting control. The lever is a credible threat to build elsewhere, and the frame is stewardship, never money.

### 13. xAI and consolidating the empire, 2023–2026 [verified unless marked]
- **Problem.** Frontier AI needs tens of billions in compute, and xAI started years behind.
- **His move.** Build compute at maximum speed, then move capital between the companies he controls.
- **His stated reasoning.**
  - He would rather be a participant than a spectator (YC 2025).
  - On diverting GPUs from Tesla in 2024: "that was in Tesla's interest, not contrary to Tesla's interest" [O].
  - On Tesla investing in xAI: "We just had a lot of investors ask us to do this." [O, Q4 2025]
- **Capital moves.**
  - Colossus brought ~100k GPUs online in ~122 days [reported]. A claimed 91 days for Colossus 2 is [unverified].
  - X merged into xAI on Mar 28, 2025, at $80B for xAI and $33B for X.
  - SpaceX put $2B into xAI (July 2025).
  - A Tesla shareholder proposal to invest in xAI got more yes votes than no on Nov 6, 2025, but abstentions sank it. Tesla announced $2B in xAI Series E preferred anyway, with its Q4 2025 results on Jan 28, 2026.
  - The $20B Series E closed Jan 6, 2026 at ~$230B.
  - SpaceX bought xAI all-stock on Feb 2, 2026, for ~$1.25T combined, the largest M&A deal on record. His post: "To the stars! @SpaceX & @xAI are now one company" [P].
  - Tesla then reported a ~$1B mark-to-market gain on SpaceX holdings (Q2 2026).
  - Related-party flows continue: SpaceX buys Megapacks, Grok runs in Teslas, and Terafab is co-built and needs "both boards." He deferred a merger question to "the appropriate process."
- **Economics and caveats.**
  - The AI segment lost about $6.4B on $3.2B of revenue in 2025 [verified].
  - The S-1 says Anthropic agreed to pay ~$1.25B a month for Colossus I and II through May 2029, terminable by either side on 90 days' notice, with reduced fees during the 2026 ramp. Musk posted on May 28, 2026 that SpaceX had committed only to a 180-day lease. **State it as a contract through May 2029, terminable on 90 days, which Musk calls a six-month lease** [verified].
  - All 11 non-Musk co-founders were gone by late March 2026. The new president's memo set the goal to "match performance of Claude" [verified].
- **Lesson.** A cash-generating entity funds the cash-burning bet, and entities he controls merge at valuations he largely sets. He will sell spare capacity even to a rival, then downplay the commitment.

### 14. The SpaceX IPO (June 2026) [verified]
- **Problem.** AI and Starship capex outgrew internal cash.
- **His move.** He reversed a 13-year stance (Tier 1, 2013) and listed SpaceX as SPCX, with super-voting shares.
- **His stated reasoning (Dimon interview, SEC-filed).**
  - "We've been cash flow positive since around 2014 or 2015 and have been self-funding." [O]
  - "So I guess the TLDR, would be: we're embarking on a massive new growth phase and we need capital for that." [O]
- **Result.**
  - **Pricing.** $135 on June 11. On June 12 it opened at $150 and closed at $160.95 (+19.2%). ~$75B raised, the largest IPO ever, for a ~$2.1T market cap.
  - **Control.** Musk holds about 82–85% of the votes (85.1% per the S-1 via Reuters; ~82.4% after the offering per the final prospectus) and 93.6% of Class B, and only Class B holders can remove him. SpaceX is a "controlled company" and carries no key-person insurance on him.
  - **Pay.** His pay is written around the mission. The SpaceX CEO Award (15 tranches, $500B→$7.5T) also requires "a permanent human colony on Mars with at least one million inhabitants." The AI award requires space data centers delivering "100 terawatts of compute per year."
  - **Stock.** SPCX peaked at $225.64 (June 16), fell to ~$105–110 in July, and was back near $160–175 in October.
- **First call (Aug 4, 2026).** Revenue was $7.8B (+92%), with a $541M net loss. The tone was friendly. He gave analysts plug numbers, turned the CFO's $100B ARR target into a floor, and pulled $1T annual revenue forward to 2030 [O].
- **Lesson.** He drops a long-held position when the mission's capital needs change, but never gives up control to do it.

### Prediction rules (case studies)
- **If unit cost must fall with scale:** he enters high and goes down-market. *High; Roadster 2006, the Mars ticket path.*
- **If refused control of something he funds:** he exits fast, withdraws the offer, and builds or buys from outside. *High; OpenAI 2017, Twitter board 2022.*
  - Counter: at Tesla the 2024 threat to "build outside" has been used as leverage for years without an exit.
- **If conditions turn after he signs:** expect a principled-sounding gating condition, not an admission of remorse. *Medium; Twitter 2022.*
- **If a cash-generating unit sits beside a cash-burning bet he controls:** he routes capital between them, framed as mission or investor demand. *High; 5 instances: SolarCity, X→xAI, SpaceX→xAI, Tesla→xAI, xAI→SpaceX.*
- **If a long-held public position blocks the capital the mission needs:** he reverses without flagging it. *Medium-high; 4 instances: the IPO, the Moon, advertising, politics.*

## Part I. How he picks markets, and where copying him bites

**Selection criteria**
1. Importance to civilization: energy, transport, space, AI, communications.
2. A physics floor far below price, protected by cost-plus contracts, dealer laws or complacency.
3. Counterfactual impact. He says Tesla sped up the EV shift by "a decade" (TED 2017).
4. If he fears a technology, he builds it rather than watch.
5. Compounding across companies: Starlink funds xAI, Grok manages Optimus, Megapacks power data centers.
6. The limiting factor of the moment, named out loud: cells, then chips and power, then memory (Tier 1, 2026).

He warned first-time founders away from capital-heavy ventures (Stanford 2003). He picked Starlink explicitly *as* a cash engine.

**Failure modes to include in answers**
- Schedule optimism on multi-step goals (Part G).
- Taste over market data. The Cybertruck reservation claim far exceeded sales, and he has said "Even if it doesn't affect sales, I want it to be beautiful." [R, Rolling Stone 2017]
- Overpaying: he bought Twitter for $44B; it was folded into xAI at $33B, and he has called the price "way overpriced".
- Brand damage from his persona, which his CFO acknowledged and he did not.
- Abrupt cuts he later reverses: the Supercharger team, and Twitter's "babies thrown out with the bathwater" [O, CNBC 2023].
- Related-party governance: SolarCity, the xAI investments, Megapack sales to SpaceX.
- Survivorship bias. He gave SpaceX and Tesla under 10% odds (Tier 1).
