### 1.10 What's true as of Oct 2026

**Priorities, in the order he talks about them**
1. **AI compute at SpaceX.** SpaceX is public (SPCX) and has absorbed xAI and X. Targets: >2 GW of compute by end-2026, 20 GW of power planned for end-2027 (he expects about 15), space data centers, Terafab, and memory as "the limiting factor."
2. **Optimus and autonomy at Tesla.**
   - Fremont Optimus production is "soon."
   - Cybercab production has started and is slow.
   - Unsupervised robotaxis run in Austin, Dallas and Houston (small fleets; crowd-sourced counts of about 37–46 in Austin [unverified]).
   - Unsupervised FSD for customer cars is promised "probably" in Q4 2026.
   - Q3 earnings are due Oct 21, 2026.
3. **Starship V3 cadence and the Moon.** Flight 14 reached orbit. Next: ship catch, about a flight a day by around Aug 2027, human-rating by end-2027.
4. **The midterms.** He is likely the largest donor of the 2026 cycle so far (Reuters, Oct 12, 2026).

**Positions now**
- **AI:** 80–90% chance it goes well. Smarter than any single human by 2026–27. Answer to job loss: universal high income through federal checks.
- **Space:** Moon first because it is faster; Mars later.
- **Speech and media:** "legacy media lies"; Grok and Grokipedia as counter-sources.
- **Self-label:** "centrist," "small government." In practice he funds Republicans.
- **Trump:** cordial (the Jan 2026 dinner).
- **Lawsuits:** OpenAI appeal vowed, no ruling. In the pending NAACP suit alleging Clean Air Act violations at xAI's Southaven plant, the DOJ intervened on xAI's side.

**Money and control**
- **Forbes made him the first trillionaire at the June IPO** (about $1.1T). He peaked around $1.3T (Bloomberg) and fell to about $719B in the late-July low (Benzinga). SPCX recovered to about $162–175 in early October, but no October net-worth figure is verified.

**Posture**

- Fewer near-term unit counts. More "S-curve," "impossible to predict" and "flat and long," while the mega-targets stay (Optimus 4 at 10M a year; $1T SpaceX revenue in 2030).
- More regulatory caution in his wording ("we don't want to jump the gun on regulators").
- Posting about 45–55 times a day.
- Calls himself "more chill," yet still retaliates against the media.

### 2.10 Scorecard

The 99 rows above carry 105 outcome entries (a few rows have more than one): **7 Hit, 6 Partly, 56 Missed, 32 Pending**, 3 Unchecked, 1 Contested. Of the 69 resolved entries, about one in ten was a hit; the hits are all single-step engineering milestones (FSD beta, Optimus prototype, Model 3 start, 5k a week, 2020 growth, tower catch, storage growth). ### 2.11 Pattern analysis

**1. He hits near-term steps he controls himself.** Evidence: FSD beta in "a month or so" (2020), the booster catch at "80 90%" (2024), the Optimus prototype (2022), Model 3 start (2017), 5k a week (2018), Cybercab start (Apr 2026). The common factor is one engineering step, inside his companies, months away. (G; C.)

**2. He misses dated multi-step and volume goals, usually by 1–6 years.** Examples: autonomy every year from 2016 to 2025, Optimus unit counts, Dojo, 4680, the $25k car, the Roadster (about 8 years), Semi (about 7), Mars windows, Neuralink (2–6). Volume forecasts after 2022 failed in both 2024 and 2025. Capability claims tend to arrive eventually; dated volume claims seldom do. (G, 10 Tesla calls; Wiki list.)

**3. He re-scopes metrics silently.** In ten 2024–26 Tesla calls he never says "we missed X."
- Robotaxi coverage: half the US population → 8–10 metros → a quarter to half the US → "a dozen or so states" → weekly unsupervised miles.
- Optimus: "thousands in factories" → "R&D phase."
- The $25k car → a "Standard" trim plus Cybercab.

After misses he switches from unit counts to S-curve fatalism ("It is impossible to predict," Q1 2026; "flat and long," Q2 2026). Long-dated mega-numbers survive the switch. (Tesla calls 2024–26.)

**4. He admits optimism as a category, then gives a new date.** The admission is a ritual that comes before the next forecast, not instead of one:
- "I know I'm the boy who cried FSD" (Q2 2023)
- "my predictions on this have been overly optimistic in the past" (Q2 2024)
- "pathologically optimistic but I do deliver in the end" (2024 meeting)
- "there's a damn wolf this time" (Q4 2024)
- "We are not always on time, but we get it done" (Q2 2025)
- "I know I've said that a few times, but" (2025 meeting)

When challenged, he points to the hedge ("No, I didn't say we will. I said, I think." CNBC 2023) and to eventual delivery. He separates "delusional" (admitted) from "fake" (denied). He calls internal schedules deliberately maximal, Parkinson's "law of gaseous expansion." He adds that "the media reports on when I'm late but never reports when I'm early" (Q4 2024). (G, C, D, A: 4 source sets.)

**5. He pads some targets on purpose.**
- The 2026 SpaceX power plan is 20 GW, and he says he expects about 15 because about a quarter of projects run late.
- At Investor Day 2023 he rounded a ~$6T calculation up to $10T ("we're being conservative").
- In 2026 he turns the CFO's revenue target into a floor ("not a question mark").

This is a management method as well as optimism: aim high so the slipped result still beats a conservative plan. (SpaceX Q2 2026 call; Investor Day 2023: 2 explicit sources.)

**Counter-examples and conditions.**
- **Friendly, capital-raising settings bring more caution.** In SpaceX 2026 he stacks "probably," "tentatively," and "assuming we receive regulatory approval." Pre-labels like "I know this sounds totally nuts" grow after 2023.
- **Engineering candor comes in the same breath as hype.** "Probably not" on his own 10k Optimus plan; HW3 "simply does not have the capability."
- **He does give ground on cheap things** (FSD transfers, advertising), never on dated strategy.

**Prediction rules: track record**
- **For any "Elon-style" deadline the skill writes,** say he would set it at roughly a third to half of the realistic time (an assumption; he states no ratio). Single-step goals he controls tend to land; multi-step or volume goals tend to slip 1–6 years. *Medium* (~100 rows; G).
- **Asked about a missed target,** he will (1) make a self-deprecating joke or a category admission, (2) explain the mechanism (S-curve, the slowest supplier, regulators), (3) say "late, but I deliver," and (4) give a new date or a new metric. He will not itemize the miss. *High* (C, D, G).
- **When he needs a forecast to motivate,** he states a specific number right after a hedge ("These are just guesses"). Report both halves. *High* (G).
- **Long horizons get bolder after misses, not humbler.** Mars went from 40–100 years to about 20; Optimus from 1M to 10M a year. *Medium-High* (2 chains).
